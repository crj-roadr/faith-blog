# Devotional Colossians 2: Freedom in Christ and the End of Legalism

Today's devotional is found in the book of Colossians, chapter 2, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Colossae, explaining to them how those who accepted Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior were delivered from the chains of the Law. That is, many of the religious precepts, rituals, ceremonies, and teachings no longer apply to Christians.

## Observations

1. **Freedom in Christ.** Paul warns the Colossians—but this warning also applies to us—that they must be very careful with anyone who is extremely religious regarding precepts, laws, customs, ordinances, and religious teachings. Because despite seeming very diligent in following all customs, ordinances, and religious ceremonies, they are clinging very closely to religious pride and the standards of men. Whereas the priority should be keeping the commandments, leading life with love, and proclaiming the gospel of Christ. The problem with people who cling to ordinances such as circumcision, diet, and ceremonial purity is that they end up disobeying and rejecting the sacrifice of Christ without realizing it. For Christ came precisely to set us free from the chains of the Law that imprisoned us in a prison of sin where we were condemned to death. For the Law came to guide us in the way of holiness and restrain our propensity to sin until the coming of Christ (Galatians 3:19, 24). First, because we had no way to fulfill all the Law of God due to our sinful nature. Second, because we were using these laws, precepts, and ordinances to boast of our religiosity relative to others. That is, we were using the Law itself to exalt ourselves and despise our neighbor. And this was not the purpose of the Law. Moreover, when someone decides to follow the old precepts of the Law (such as circumcision), they reject the sacrifice of Christ and are obligated to keep the whole Law (Galatians 5:3). Therefore, Jesus came into the world to set us free from the yoke of slavery, because we would never be able to fulfill all the Law and be justified on our own (Galatians 5:1, Romans 3:20). Christ came to put an end to the legalism of the Law and bring righteousness to everyone who believes in Him (Romans 10:4). God chose to do things this way so that we would be saved by faith and not by the Law (Galatians 2:16). For only in this way can no one boast about their salvation, because we all receive it as a gift by accepting Christ as our Lord and Savior (Romans 3:23–24). Furthermore, when we believe in Christ, we are no longer under the Law but under grace (Romans 6:14). And because of Christ, we owe nothing to anyone except to love one another, for whoever loves their neighbor has fulfilled the Law (Romans 13:8). Therefore, no one can judge us by what we eat, drink, or how many festival days we celebrate, etc. (Colossians 2:16). However, this freedom we now have in Christ does not mean we can do whatever we want. It is completely the opposite. It is out of our love and gratitude for Christ that we must follow His example and lead a life of holiness to honor His sacrifice (2 Corinthians 5:15). In addition, our freedom must be used with wisdom so that it does not become a stumbling block for others (Luke 17:1–2). For when we possess more spiritual maturity than others, we must protect them from snares that they do not know could cause them to stumble in their walk of holiness. Therefore, there are certain things that can be done by Christians as long as they know the proper measure and do not compromise their faith and purpose in Christ—e.g., drinking alcohol, going out to parties, watching movies/series or listening to music that is not pure, eating pork, etc. (1 Corinthians 6:12, 10:23). For nothing is unclean in itself, but to the one who considers it unclean, to that person it is unclean (Romans 14:14). However, many of these things that may not seem unclean to us might seem so in the conscience of our neighbor. Therefore, we should avoid practicing these things in public before others so as not to cause them to sin against God (Romans 14:13, 20–21). These actions should be kept private when we are alone or in the company of others who have the spiritual maturity to know what is permissible. Therefore, we should always seek to act for the good of others, protect them from stumbling, and refrain from doing things that satisfy us if they could lead our neighbor into sin (1 Corinthians 10:23–24).
