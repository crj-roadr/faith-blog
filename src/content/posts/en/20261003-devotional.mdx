# Devotional Ephesians 6: The Armour of God and Household Duties

Today's devotional is found in the book of Ephesians, chapter 6, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul concludes his letter addressed to the churches of Ephesus, bidding farewell and offering his final instructions. Paul explained the duties of children to their parents and of parents to their children. Furthermore, he added to these the duties of bondservants and masters. Finally, he urged them to remain clothed with the armour of God (strengthening their faith), keeping alert and persevering in prayer for all the saints.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Children obey your parents, and parents do not provoke your children.** This is an observation that is not always easy to fulfill, neither for children nor for parents. From the perspective of children, depending on the relationship they have with their parents (e.g., loving, abusive, etc.), resentment can sometimes exist on the part of the children. Especially when parents struggle with vices, mistreat their children, and make poor life choices (e.g., alcoholism, drug addiction, etc.), which makes it very difficult for children to obey this commandment of God. However, we must always remember that even if our parents were not good or ideal, we must always honour them through obedience, provided they do not lead us to sin against God. For this is God's commandment and the first one accompanied by a promise: God asks children to obey their parents so that it may go well with them and that their days on earth may be prolonged (Ephesians 6:1–3). Therefore, as children, we must do so, for this is right according to God's will. Nevertheless, God also commands parents not to exasperate or provoke their children to anger. Being parents does not give them the right to act arbitrarily to the point of frustrating their children. Quite the opposite: parents are the heads of their children. Therefore, they must lead with love in an exemplary manner. Parents bear the responsibility to bring up their children in the training and instruction of the Lord (Ephesians 6:4), so that they will not depart from God's way as they grow older (Proverbs 22:6).
2. **Bondservants serve your masters wholeheartedly as unto the Lord, and masters treat your bondservants with love.** This is a controversial passage today, especially when heard or read for the first time. Many who read it quickly conclude that God endorses and supports slavery. That is the conclusion reached by many when reading without the broader biblical context of God's overarching will. But that is an incorrect interpretation, for God does not endorse or defend slavery. From the beginning, God created us to live in love with Him and with all human beings. That is why Jesus said the two greatest commandments are to love God with all our being and to love our neighbour as ourselves, for on these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets (Matthew 22:37–40). And the only way to demonstrate genuine love for God is by accepting His only-begotten Son (Jesus Christ) as our Lord and Saviour and keeping His commandments (John 14:21). When we place our faith in Jesus Christ, we receive the Holy Spirit, who grants us spiritual gifts and fruits to do God's will (Ephesians 1:13–14). It is God's will that we cease gratifying the desires of the flesh and begin living according to the desires of the Spirit. Naturally, in a situation of servitude, our flesh tempts us to hate, argue, fight, and rebel against our master. But the Spirit of God leads us to serve with love, humility, joy, patience, and self-control (Galatians 5:16–26). That said, God is fundamentally opposed to slavery because it contradicts His commandments of love and dignity. What happened historically is that God, recognizing the hardness of human hearts and the fact that civil laws of ancient nations permitted slavery, instituted regulations to limit abuse, protect dignity, and keep cruelty in check (Exodus 21:2, 16, 26–27; Deuteronomy 15:12–14; Leviticus 25:39–40). His ultimate plan was always to liberate us from sin and unite us all as equals in Christ (Galatians 3:28). For this reason, Paul wrote to Philemon to receive back his runaway servant, Onesimus, no longer as a slave, but as a beloved brother in the Lord (Philemon 1:15–16). When living under the civil laws of an earthly nation, we are called to respect legitimate authority insofar as it does not compel us to violate God's laws—giving to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's (Matthew 22:21). Yet God equally commands masters to treat their servants kindly and justly, knowing that both have the same Master in heaven, with whom there is no favouritism (Ephesians 6:9).
3. **Put on the whole armour of God to stand against the schemes of the devil.** This observation provides the true spiritual perspective we must constantly keep in mind during our earthly journey. Paul identifies who our real enemy is in this life. According to Paul, our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against spiritual forces of evil led by Satan (Ephesians 6:12). Therefore, our best protection and weapon against the tactics, strategies, and schemes of the enemy is the armour of God. That is, we must be completely clothed in God's armour to withstand and overcome the enemy's temptations. This means being continually vigilant, drawing near to God through knowledge and wisdom in His Word, praising Him, standing firm in faith, and praying without ceasing. These are our weapons against the adversary, alongside our commitment to live by the Spirit and proclaim the gospel of Christ. Only when we are fully dressed in the whole armour of God can we stand firm to the end against all the wiles of Satan (Ephesians 6:13).
