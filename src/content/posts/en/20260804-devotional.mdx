# Devotional Acts 25: The Appeal to Caesar and God's Plan

Today's devotional is found in the book of Acts, chapter 25, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** This chapter opens with Porcius Festus, the new governor of Caesarea, traveling to Jerusalem, where the high priests and Jewish religious leaders requested that he transfer Paul and his case to Jerusalem so he could preside over the tribunal there. However, this was all a pretext to ambush and kill Paul along the way. Festus did not consent and made a counterproposal: he invited them to appear before the tribunal in Caesarea, where Paul would be tried. Thus, during the hearing before the Jews, Paul was accused once again, but even the accusers themselves could not articulate the specific charges against him. Nevertheless, Festus, wishing to curry favor with the Jews, proposed transferring Paul's case to Jerusalem. Paul rejected the proposal, knowing the Jews had no legitimate charges and refusing to be handed over by officials seeking to appease the Jews for political expediency. Instead, Paul appealed to have his case transferred to Rome to be judged by Caesar, a request Festus granted. Before sending him, Festus sought the counsel of King Agrippa, who had arrived to visit Festus along with his wife, Bernice. When Festus presented Paul's case, King Agrippa expressed a desire to hear Paul himself to better understand the matter. Ultimately, Festus asked those present (especially King Agrippa) for assistance in drafting an official letter detailing the charges, explaining to Caesar the rationale for transferring Paul's case to Rome.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **God's will always prevails in the end.** It is striking to observe throughout all the preceding chapters and devotionals that the enemies never relent. The same pattern unfolds in this chapter: the Jews took advantage of Festus's visit to Jerusalem to plot yet another ambush against Paul with the intent to kill him (Acts 25:2–3). They could not even substantiate the accusations they leveled against Paul, nor did they possess any evidence against him (Acts 25:7). It was merely their word against Paul's, and unevidenced testimony was insufficient to convict him. Even so, Paul was not released because the governors of Caesarea sought to accommodate the Jews, keeping Paul imprisoned to maintain favorable relations with them (Acts 24:7 and Acts 25:10–11). This understandably caused Paul great distress and hardship, as he remained innocently confined. Yet his comfort rested in the words of Jesus Christ, knowing Jesus had promised that believers would face persecution for their faith just as He did (John 15:18–20). Furthermore, Paul maintained hope because he knew Jesus had overcome the world (John 16:33) and that God's sovereign will always prevails (Ephesians 1:11). Despite all these schemes against God's purpose, the Jews could not thwart God's will. They failed to secure Paul's transfer to Jerusalem; instead, he would be sent to Rome to stand trial before Caesar (Acts 25:12). Regardless of our trials, we must always remember that we are blessed when persecuted for righteousness' sake (Matthew 5:10–12) and that God secures the ultimate victory.
