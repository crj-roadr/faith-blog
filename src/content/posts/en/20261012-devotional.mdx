# Devotional Colossians 4: Wisdom in Conversation and Prayer

Today's devotional is found in the book of Colossians, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul concludes his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Colossae, instructing them to persevere in prayer with thanksgiving, to pray for those who risk their lives to preach the gospel of Christ, and to live wisely toward those who do not believe in Jesus Christ—making the most of every opportunity to always converse in a gracious, pleasant, and tasteful manner.

## Observations

1. **Living and conversing wisely with those who do not believe in Christ, so that conversation is always gracious and pleasant.** This is a key principle that we as Christians must follow. For, whether we realize it or not, how we behave and treat our neighbor directly affects how people receive and interpret the gospel (and, consequently, receive Christ). For although we can never represent Christ worthily, we are His followers and advocates here on earth. Therefore, our conduct will influence how our neighbor evaluates Jesus. On the one hand, it is not fair for people to use His followers to criticize and reject Christ, because what is just is to know Christ first (e.g., His beliefs, His conduct, what He preached, etc.) and only then criticize poor followers. But in reality, that is not how things happen. Most people criticize Christians, and because of them, even without knowing Christ, they reject Him. Yet, on the other hand, it is understandable why they adopt this attitude. For if this person is so wonderful, with all these qualities and teachings that Christians claim, why do Christians not represent Him well? Our rational brain searches for consistency between beliefs and behavior. If they are not aligned, then we do not truly believe what we preach. In this sense, they have a point and we must admit it. That is why Paul stated that it is because of our fault as Christians who fail to represent Christ properly that unbelievers blaspheme the name of God and reject Him (Romans 2:17–24). It is for this reason that Paul warns the believers in Colossae, and we as believers must take this same warning with the utmost seriousness. Because the last thing we want is to be responsible for driving both unbelievers and believers away from Christ through our conduct (Luke 17:1–2). Therefore, so that we do not become stumbling blocks to others, we must do the following. First, ask God to give us wisdom and understanding in His Word to know how to discern good conduct from bad. Second, ask God so that, through His Holy Spirit dwelling in us, we develop the gifts and fruit of the Spirit (Romans 12:6–8; 1 Corinthians 12:7–11; Galatians 5:22–23). Third, we must put into practice the two greatest commandments according to Jesus Christ—to love God with all our being and to love our neighbor as ourselves (Matthew 22:36–40). Finally, we must remain watchful for opportune moments that will arise to share the gospel of Christ with our neighbor. But when the opportunity comes, we must put everything we have learned into practice so we can converse with love, wisdom, understanding, empathy, respect, and good faith. So that the experience is enriching and transformative for both. For only in this way will we properly represent Christ and draw our neighbor closer to Him.
