# Devotional Hebrews 7: Jesus, the High Priest in the Order of Melchizedek

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 7, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining to them why Jesus is a High Priest in the order of Melchizedek.

## Observations

1. **Jesus is like Melchizedek because His priesthood possesses three distinct characteristics: He became a priest not by human ancestry but by the power of an indestructible life, He was appointed not without an oath but with an oath from God, and He has no need to offer daily sacrifices for Himself or for others.** First, Jesus became High Priest by the power of His holy life. That is, He was not chosen to be High Priest because He belonged to an earthly lineage charged with priestly duties. In fact, the opposite is true: Jesus descended not from Levi, but from Judah—a tribe from which no one served at the altar. Technically, He had no genealogical claim to the priesthood. Yet, on the basis of His blameless character and the power of an indestructible life, He was designated High Priest forever (Hebrews 7:15–16). Second, Jesus was established as High Priest confirmed by the solemn oath of God the Father. This sets Him completely apart from all Levitical high priests, who were appointed merely by ancestral law without an oath. Thus, Jesus has become the guarantor of a better hope and a far superior covenant with God (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:20–22). Third, Jesus set aside the former regulation because it was weak and unprofitable, bringing nothing to perfection. More importantly, He introduced a better hope through which we draw near to God. Jesus supersedes the old ceremonial law because He never needs to offer sacrifices for His own sins, having lived a sinless, blameless life. Likewise, there is no longer any need for ongoing daily sacrifices for the sins of the people, because Christ offered Himself once for all when He offered up the perfect sacrifice. In other words, through His spotless purity, sinless life, and permanent priesthood, He became the ultimate sacrifice to atone for all our sins—for all who have ever lived, who live today, and who will ever live (Hebrews 7:23–27). It is for all these reasons that He is our supreme Lord and Savior, wholly worthy of our unwavering faith.
