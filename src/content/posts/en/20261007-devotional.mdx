# Devotional Philippians 3: Trust in Christ and the Example to Follow

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philippians, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all the believers in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, explaining to them that they must place their full trust in Jesus Christ and not in men. Furthermore, he also exhorts them to follow the example that he himself gave them and of all those who have the same conduct worthy of a Christian.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **We must place all our trust in Jesus Christ.** I know it is hard to genuinely believe when such a statement is made. Especially when we place our trust in ourselves or other people. And being honest, I can see why this is the case when we do not have an intimate relationship with God. For in the era we live in, we are no longer in the Garden of Eden and we do not physically cohabit in the same place as God. Because Adam and Eve sinned against God and disobeyed Him. Consequently, they were expelled from the presence of God (Genesis 3:11–23). That is why it is very difficult to have total trust in God, because now we cannot see Him, touch Him, or hear Him physically. Because we are beings much more connected with the material world than the spiritual world—which is where God and celestial creatures dwell. However, as we go through life and perceive that human beings are flawed and should never be the object of total trust for all of humanity, that is when we understand that only God is worthy of occupying this place in our lives. For man is corrupt, selfish, manipulative, treacherous, and all the other bad things that we all repudiate. Furthermore, man is not all-powerful, not eternal, and not omniscient. However, only God possesses all these characteristics (omnipotence, omnipresence, omniscience, and eternity). Therefore, only He is capable of saving us from death (through resurrection) and worthy of being at the center of our lives.
2. **We must follow the example of Christ and all those who have a similar conduct.** This is another observation that is very difficult to put into practice for two reasons. First, our sinful nature does not naturally allow us to live life according to Christ's example. We are the fruit of thousands of generations of sinners, so we are biologically programmed to sin. That is, living in sin is what comes most naturally to us. Second, when we decide to embark on this walk of holiness, we face the rejection of the world. For as Jesus already told us, if the world hates us, we must remember that the world first hated Christ. For the world rejects what is not its own. And since we are no longer of the world when we follow Jesus Christ, the world rejects us too (John 15:18–19). That is, when we copy Christ's conduct, people call us foolish for not living in "reality" because, according to them, "God does not exist." In other words, our life is much more complicated and we run the risk of being persecuted, depending on the country where we live, for believing and preaching the gospel of Christ. However, despite all this, it is the best decision we can make in our lives. For when we put things in perspective, we realize that we are eternal beings. That is, the time we have here is a fraction of seconds relative to the eternity that awaits us. For everyone, whether they want to or not, will be part of eternity. The only difference is the location where we will live our eternity (paradise or hell). It is up to each one to choose what they prefer—please men and displease God, or please God and displease men? The decision is ours.
