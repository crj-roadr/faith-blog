# Devotional Ephesians 4: Unity in Christ and Living as Children of Light

Today's devotional is found in the book of Ephesians, chapter 4, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Ephesus, explaining to them how they must be united in Christ and honour the name that we all received as Christians of the body of Christ. Furthermore, Paul also insists that they stop living to satisfy the desires of the flesh and begin living to satisfy the desires of the Spirit.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Unity in the body of Christ.** Paul explains to the Ephesians that they must strive to be tolerant of one another. That they must always be gentle, patient, and humble with one another, for we are all part of the same body—the body of Christ. Thanks to Jesus' sacrifice, we were all united in one faith, one body, and one Spirit (Ephesians 4:1–6). Therefore, when we fight, argue, and fuel enmity, we are divided as members of the same body and fail to fulfill our purposes. For when Jesus returned to heaven to be at the right hand of God the Father, He sent the Holy Spirit to comfort and guide us until the final judgment (John 15:26 and 16:7). And when we receive the Holy Spirit, He distributes spiritual gifts to each of us (1 Corinthians 12:7–11). In other words, we all have purposes here on earth and receive spiritual gifts to fulfill God's will. Therefore, when we are divided among brothers and sisters (members of the body of Christ), we hinder our mission, strengthen the enemy, and push others away from God's grace.
2. **Live as children of light.** This is the warning Paul gives to the Ephesians who have already received the gospel of Jesus. Because many, due to the life they led before knowing Jesus, were lost in their ignorance and the hardness of their hearts. People with this type of conduct have their understanding darkened regarding the Word of God and, consequently, distance themselves further and further from the life that comes from God (Ephesians 4:17–18). For knowledge and wisdom in the Holy Scriptures and all other spiritual gifts come from the Holy Spirit of God. Therefore, when we live as children of darkness, our hearts harden because the Holy Spirit cannot dwell in a body that lives to satisfy the desires of the flesh. Thus, the more indulgent we are toward the works of the flesh, the less we hear the voice of the Spirit and the less we are influenced by Him. That is why Paul mentions that the desires of the flesh and the desires of the Spirit oppose each other. One cannot grow while the other dominates. In other words, they cannot coexist—one must yield for the other to flourish (Galatians 5:16–17). It is for this reason that Paul insists on warning them to stop living as children of darkness and begin living as children of light. This same warning applies to us who have already heard the gospel of Christ yet continue to do the opposite. Our conduct must change because it is through our fruit that it will be known whether we or anyone else (children included) have truly accepted Christ as our Lord and Saviour (Matthew 7:16–20 and Proverbs 20:11). For conduct worthy of a Christian reflects the fruits of the Spirit, which are love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. These things are holy, and against such things there is no law (Galatians 5:22–23). Therefore, as Christians, Paul asks the Ephesians to embrace these qualities in their lives, and we must do the same.
