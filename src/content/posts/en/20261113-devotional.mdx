# Devotional Hebrews 12: God's Discipline and the Danger of Rejecting Him

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 12, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining to them why God disciplines His children and the danger of rejecting God.

## Observations

1. **God disciplines His children just as parents discipline their children.** This is an observation and analogy that we should carry with us. For God calls us to be disciplined and to follow the path of holiness because He loves us and wants what is best for us — just like our parents do. But unlike our parents, who have a limited perspective of the universe and the future, God knows everything. Therefore, when He tells us we must do something because it is better for us, we would be foolish not to follow His guidance. If we obey our parents, who have a vision that is limited relative to God's, why would we not obey God, who is the creator of the universe and the most powerful and wise entity there is?

2. **We must be careful not to reject God, for if those who refused the one who warned them on earth did not escape, how much less will we escape if we turn away from the one who warns us from heaven.** This is a warning that, though directed at the Hebrews of that era, also applies to us. For there is no salvation without believing in God. If we are looking for excuses to reject God and disobey Him, we must remind ourselves that our transgressions affect not only our lives here on earth — they also carry spiritual dimensions. For our time here on earth is very short compared to the eternity that awaits us, whether it be a good or a bad eternity. Intuitively, we understand the consequences of our transgressions here on earth: we know that fighting with friends is different from fighting with the police, which is different from fighting against the president, because the gravity of the consequences grows greater as we transgress against ever-higher authorities. And God is the supreme authority of the universe. Therefore, transgressing His boundaries is infinitely worse than transgressing the limits of any earthly leader. Furthermore, we must remember that Jesus came to save us because we are incorrigible sinners — we have all sinned against God and no one can atone for their own sins by themselves (Galatians 2:21). Therefore, the question we must answer is this: is it wise to reject a gift that God has freely given us, undeserved, for our own salvation? This is the most important question that all of us must answer before the return of Christ.
