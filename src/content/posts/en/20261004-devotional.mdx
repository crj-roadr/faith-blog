# Devotional Philippians 1: How Persecution Spreads the Gospel

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philippians, chapter 1, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to all the believers in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, explaining to them how all the persecution that led to his imprisonment in Rome has actually helped advance the gospel of Christ. For the entire palace guard and everyone else have come to know that he is imprisoned solely for his faith in Jesus Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **The persecution of Christians for the sake of Christ contributes to the spreading of the gospel.** This statement may sound somewhat contradictory at first, but it is reality. In our minds, we rarely view suffering persecution for a cause as a catalyst that helps spread that very same cause. Yet upon deeper reflection, we can clearly see how it fosters the propagation of the message. The moment people learn that someone is being persecuted for a belief, curiosity is aroused: why are they persecuting this person for believing this? This is precisely what Paul conveys to the Philippians when he explains that everything that happened to him for trusting Jesus as the Messiah has served to advance the gospel of Christ. The main reason Paul points to is that people throughout the imperial guard and beyond have recognized his imprisonment is for Christ. When believers see all the hardships Paul has endured while still maintaining bold hope and joy, they grow more confident in the Lord and dare to speak the Word of God without fear (Philippians 1:12–14). Nevertheless, Paul also clarifies that not everyone who helps spread the gospel does so with pure motives. He identifies two kinds of preachers: those who preach Christ out of selfish ambition, and those who preach out of love. The former act out of self-interest rather than a genuine desire to help others; they are driven by envy, rivalry, the pursuit of fame, money, or the wish to stir up trouble for faithful preachers. In contrast, those who proclaim the gospel out of love seek to help others receive salvation with no personal agenda. Yet even so, both groups contribute to making Christ known, and for that reason we can rejoice as Christians even when circumstances are far from ideal (Philippians 1:15–18). Furthermore, we must remember that if we truly live by the Spirit and do the will of God, we should expect persecution, for a servant is not greater than his master. If Jesus Christ—who is sinless and infinitely greater than us in all spiritual gifts and fruits—was opposed, persecuted, and crucified, who are we to expect an easier path? Jesus explicitly told His disciples that a servant is not greater than his master, and that in this world they would face persecution and trials (John 15:20). Scripture also indicates that facing reproach for the name of Christ is a sign that the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon us (1 Peter 4:14). Moreover, Jesus reassured us that when we are brought before authorities or questioned for our faith, we need not fear how to defend ourselves, for the Holy Spirit will give us the right words at that very moment (Matthew 10:19–20). Whenever we suffer persecution in Christ's name, we should rejoice, knowing that our reward in heaven is great (Matthew 5:10–12). Finally, Christ asked us to have peace and take heart even in the midst of trials, because He has overcome the world and we share in His victory (John 16:33). Therefore, whenever we face opposition for believing in Jesus Christ, we must remind ourselves that we are more than conquerors, even when external circumstances seem to suggest otherwise.
