# Devotional 1 Timothy 3: Qualifications for Church Leaders

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Timothy, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter of his first letter to his spiritual son in the faith, Timothy, Paul outlines the required character, reputation, conduct, and leadership qualities for those who desire to serve as overseers and deacons.

## Observations

1. **Bishops and deacons must be blameless, faithful to their spouses, good leaders of their households and children, respectable, and not lovers of money.** These were the instructions Paul gave to Timothy regarding the character qualifications of those fit to hold office as overseers and deacons. I wholeheartedly agree with these guidelines. Anyone who assumes a position of leadership in the church must be a faithful follower and imitator of Christ. Otherwise, rather than helping, they end up causing harm and driving people further away from God. When we attend church and listen to a religious leader teach, we rightly expect their conduct, reputation, and knowledge to be congruent with their stated beliefs. This is only fair, for no one can credibly instruct others to do what they themselves fail to practice. Even if the doctrine taught is sound, an instructor who fails to live it out becomes a hypocrite. This is something we must never do as Christians. From the moment we identify ourselves as Christians before the world, our conduct must reflect our convictions so that we do not become stumbling blocks to others. As Jesus Christ warned, it is far better to die a severe death than to cause new believers in the faith to stumble (Matthew 18:6–7). For these reasons, Paul emphasizes blameless moral integrity, a good reputation with outsiders, freedom from the love of money, and the many other vital traits church leaders must possess. Sadly, in our own day, there are many church leaders who preach distorted doctrines and lack the very character Paul prescribed. It is largely because of such figures that many people today reject the church and blaspheme the name of God (Romans 2:21–24). Therefore, we must remain vigilant and discerning regarding our spiritual leaders. The only way to discern whether their character aligns with biblical standards is by knowing the Word of God for ourselves. Without the wisdom and knowledge found in Scripture, it is easy to be misled by false doctrines. Consequently, if we discover that our leaders do not preach the true gospel or exhibit the conduct required by the Bible, we must seek another fellowship that faithfully reflects the gospel of Christ.
