# Devotional 1 Timothy 4: God's Creation and Christian Freedom

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Timothy, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter of his first letter to his spiritual son in the faith, Timothy, Paul instructs him on what to teach the flock. Paul explains that the Scriptures warn that in the latter times, some will abandon the faith to follow deceiving spirits and demonic doctrines, forbidding marriage and ordering abstinence from certain foods. But Paul clarifies that everything God created is good, provided it is received with thanksgiving. Furthermore, Paul urges Timothy to reject irreverent myths and worldly fables, focusing instead on training in godliness, which holds value for both the present life and the life to come. Finally, Paul encourages him to devote himself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, and to teaching.

## Observations

1. **We must remember that nothing God created is bad if it is received with thanksgiving.** These words of Paul clarify so much regarding our walk of holiness with Jesus Christ. When I was younger in the faith and lacked spiritual maturity, wisdom, and deep knowledge of the Word, I mistakenly assumed that certain things were intrinsically evil, even though they were part of God's creation. In many religious circles, one often heard that you shouldn't style your hair a certain way, shouldn't dress a certain way, could never drink any wine, listen to certain styles of music, or watch certain movies because they were inherently evil. As a result, I grew up associating many neutral aspects of creation as evil in themselves. However, as I began to search the Scriptures and understand the character and heart of God, I realized that Paul's affirmation is profound and true. In Genesis 1, God looked upon all that He had made and declared it very good (Genesis 1:31). Knowing that God is the only perfectly holy, omniscient, omnipresent, and omnipotent Being, it is impossible for Him to create anything evil (Deuteronomy 32:4; Matthew 5:48; Jeremiah 32:17; Luke 1:37; Psalm 139:1–4; Hebrews 4:13; Proverbs 15:3). Moreover, Christ set us free from the yoke of legalistic slavery—the burden of keeping the ceremonial law for justification—yet we must never use our freedom as a cover for sinful fleshly indulgence, but rather to love and serve God (Galatians 5:1, 13; 1 Corinthians 6:12). Sin originates from our fallen human nature, which misuses God's good gifts to gratify carnal lusts (Galatians 5:19–21). If our conscience convicts us that an activity is sinful for us, we must refrain from it, for whatever does not proceed from faith harms us. But if our conscience is clean and anchored in Scripture, what we receive with thanksgiving is sanctified by the Word of God and prayer. Above all, we must prioritize how our actions affect our brothers and sisters in Christ. It is far better to forgo eating meat, drinking wine, or engaging in any permissible liberty than to cause a fellow believer to stumble (Romans 14:14–23). Therefore, let us walk in the glorious liberty of Christ, neither abusing our freedom to satisfy carnal passions nor flaunting it to make others stumble, for neither builds up the kingdom of God.
