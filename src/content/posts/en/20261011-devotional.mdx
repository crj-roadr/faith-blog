# Devotional Colossians 3: Holy Living and God's Design for the Family

Today's devotional is found in the book of Colossians, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Colossae, explaining to them the standards to follow in order to live a holy life and the guidelines for the Christian family.

## Observations

1. **Standards for a holy life.** When we repent of our sins and accept Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior, we acknowledge that our former lives were not worthy of a servant of God. Furthermore, we also recognize that to honor the sacrifice Jesus made for us, we must follow Him faithfully and represent Him rightly in our lives. That is, we must crucify our sins on the cross with Christ and rise with new bodies and restored souls to serve Him properly (Romans 6:6–11). This means that we no longer live for ourselves, but rather Jesus Christ lives in us (Galatians 2:20). That is, we no longer live for sin, but for righteousness. Therefore, we must stop gratifying the desires of the flesh and seek to increasingly satisfy the desires of the Spirit. Since both cannot coexist, because when we feed one we starve the other (Galatians 5:16–17). Thus, there is no way to remain on the fence of indecision and try to satisfy both. Therefore, choose which of the two you will serve. However, to know which to serve, we must distinguish the works of the flesh from the fruit of the Spirit. The works of the flesh are the following: sexual immorality, greed, envy, anger, hostility, idolatry, selfishness, orgies, and many others. In contrast, the fruit of the Spirit is: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (Galatians 5:19–23). Therefore, those who belong to Christ crucify the flesh with its passions and desires to live by the Spirit (Galatians 5:24–25).
2. **Guidelines for the Christian family.** Paul also explains in this letter how the Christian family should be ordered and what the responsibilities and divine order in the home are. First, he exhorts wives to submit to their husbands as is fitting in the Lord. But he also commands husbands to love their wives as Christ loved the church and not be harsh with them. Second, he commands children to obey their parents in everything, for this pleases the Lord. However, he also warns fathers not to exasperate their children or discourage them. Finally, Paul commands servants/bondservants to obey their earthly masters in everything, not only when they are being watched as people-pleasers. For that is the attitude of one who seeks human approval. As Christians, we must do everything heartily as unto the Lord and not unto men, knowing that from the Lord we will receive the inheritance as our reward—i.e., eternal life (Colossians 3:18–25). In these verses, we see Paul explaining the divine order in the household. These passages connect with others by Paul explaining the hierarchical order where God is the head of Christ, Christ is the head of man, and man is the head of woman. That is, the wife submits to her husband, the husband submits to Christ, and Christ submits to God. Highlighting that man is the image and glory of God, while woman is the glory of man, having been created for man. That said, Paul also emphasizes that both man and woman must submit mutually to one another in Christ, remembering that everything comes from God (1 Corinthians 11:3, 7–8, 11–12).
