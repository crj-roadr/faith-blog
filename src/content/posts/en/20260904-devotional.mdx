# Devotional 1 Corinthians 12: Spiritual Gifts and the Body of Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Corinthians, chapter 12, and this was what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul explains in his letter to the Corinthians how each person has their spiritual gift and how we are all members of the body of Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Each person has their spiritual gift.** Paul explains to his Christian brothers and sisters in Corinth that we all have different gifts and that this does not mean some are better than others. On the contrary, God tends to give gifts according to each person's character, so that no one may boast and everyone recognizes that their gift comes from God. Furthermore, God gives us gifts to help others (1 Corinthians 12:7). There are various gifts of the Spirit (e.g., wisdom in the Word, knowledge of the Word, faith, the ability to heal the sick, working of miracles, prophecy, discernment of spirits, speaking in different kinds of tongues, and the interpretation of tongues) (1 Corinthians 12:8). Each of us receives one or more of these spiritual gifts for the common good of others, not to exalt ourselves. Moreover, all gifts are important and none is superior to another, precisely so that there may be no division among brothers and sisters in Christ. All gifts have their specific function and must be used to accomplish the will of God.
2. **We are all members of the body of Christ.** Paul also takes the opportunity to clarify the truth that we are all members of the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:12–13). This also means that each of us fulfills different functions (or callings). Just as a body cannot function properly without one of its members—no matter how insignificant it might seem. In reality, the parts that seem weaker or less prominent are often the most indispensable. And in the same way God designed our human bodies, He also organized us as members of the body of Jesus. That is, God gives greater honor to parts that lack it so that there should be no division among us and that we should value one another's callings equally (1 Corinthians 12:22–25). God does this so that no one can boast of themselves or think they are better than others, let alone believe their purpose was earned by their own merit (1 Corinthians 4:7 and Ephesians 2:8–9).
