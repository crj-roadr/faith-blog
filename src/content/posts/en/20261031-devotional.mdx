# Devotional Titus 3: The Believer's Conduct Toward Authorities

Today's devotional is found in the book of Titus, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul bids farewell in his letter to his son in the faith, Titus, reminding him that believers must be obedient and submissive to authorities and rulers, even when they disagree with them. For in the past, believers themselves were foolish, ignorant, slaves to various lusts and pleasures, envious, and malicious. Only when we receive the Spirit of God through the sacrifice of Jesus Christ and by accepting Him as our Lord and Savior does our conduct change. Furthermore, Paul warns him to avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, and quarrels about the law, which lead nowhere. They only serve to divide people, so such individuals should be warned and then avoided.

## Observations

1. **The believer's conduct toward authorities and rulers.** Paul reminds Titus that believers should be obedient and submissive to authorities and rulers. This is the proper conduct of a Christian toward governing powers. Although this may sound strange in our modern world of extreme political polarization and rampant intolerance, it is what is right—even when we do not agree with their attitudes and agendas. It is important to behave this way even when we dislike it, because in a sense authorities come into power because God permits it. For in reality, there is no authority except that which God has established (Romans 13:1). Therefore, disobeying authorities—as long as they are not compelling us to sin against God—is disobeying God. Moreover, our good conduct serves as an example and a shining light whose uprightness sparks the curiosity of others. We must also be careful not to judge authorities harshly, for we ourselves were once foolish and disobedient before believing in God. We were enslaved by the pleasures of the flesh, envy, and selfishness, lacking wisdom and awareness of the terrible things we did. For the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and a good understanding have all those who practice His commandments (Psalm 111:10). Furthermore, we did not attain these virtues by our own merit, but rather through the grace of God by our faith in Christ as our Lord and Savior (Titus 3:4–7). Therefore, we must also be patient with others who have not yet been transformed by the Spirit of God. We must avoid debates and disputes over matters that do not help our neighbor draw closer to God (e.g., Jewish statutes, dietary rules, genealogies, etc.). These matters no longer apply to Christians because they were fulfilled by Jesus Christ through His perfect life and sacrifice. Such quarrels only confuse and divide people, driving them further away from God (Titus 3:9–10). Therefore, submitting to authorities with obedience, love, and integrity is the true conduct of a Christian.
