# Devotional James 1: Why We Should Rejoice in Trials

Today's devotional is found in the book of James, chapter 1, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, James writes a letter addressed to the Christians of the twelve tribes of Israel scattered throughout the world, explaining to them why we are blessed when we face various trials of our faith and the importance of putting the Word into practice.

## Observations

1. **We are blessed when we face various trials of our faith, for they produce perseverance.** This is an observation that we all know to be true, yet no one delights in walking through wilderness seasons. We all know that we only discover what we truly believe when we are tested. That is why when we want to know what someone truly believes, we pose a hypothetical scenario where that person is caught between life and death and must choose between two options—the proverbial scenario where a gun is pointed at our head and we must choose. On the other hand, we also know that a person only improves in any area of life when they succeed in overcoming the difficulties they encounter along the path toward their desired future. There is no growth without resistance or adversity. Therefore, we should view tribulations as opportunities to put our faith to the test and allow it to grow.
2. **Knowing the Word without putting it into practice is deceiving oneself and is completely useless.** This is the absolute truth, and it does not apply solely to the Holy Scriptures. We know this because we all arrive at the same conclusion when we notice someone who knows a great deal about something that could transform their life, yet never puts it into practice. There are people who know an immense amount about nutrition, health, and exercise, but never apply that knowledge to their daily lives. Furthermore, there are people who earn degrees in university courses they will never use. Regarding such people, we usually say that all the knowledge they worked so hard to acquire became useless because they neither use it nor share it with others. Therefore, as Christians, it is not enough merely to know or listen to the Word. We must put it into practice. That is why the psalmist said that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and all who follow His precepts have good understanding (Psalm 111:10). This makes complete sense, because no one learns anything solely through theory. It is necessary to put what is learned into practice in order to consolidate knowledge and understand the subject through personal experience. We know this is true because no one learns mathematics, driving a car, or dancing simply by reading and absorbing knowledge without ever practicing it. Therefore, we should expect nothing different when it comes to the Word of God. We can know and listen to God's commandments, but without putting them into practice, we do not truly understand what we are learning. We are simply deceiving ourselves.
