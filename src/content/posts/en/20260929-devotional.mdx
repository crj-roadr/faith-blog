# Devotional Ephesians 2: From Death to Life and Unity in Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of Ephesians, chapter 2, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Ephesus, explaining how, because of the great love God has for us — even while we were sinners and undeserving — He showed us mercy and gave us life with Christ. That is, He raised us up with Christ so that we may live together with Him in the age to come. Paul also explains how all peoples have now been united in Christ, reconciled with God, and have inherited as fellow citizens of the people of promise the inheritance of God.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Life in Christ.** This is the most important fact in all of our lives. For before the coming of Jesus Christ into the world, we were practically dead (Genesis 2:16–17). We were so lost in sin that God had to execute the first great judgement upon humanity through the flood (Genesis 6:7, 13 and 17). However, God, being compassionate, promised never again to wipe out all living beings from the face of the earth with the waters of a flood (Genesis 9:11). He then decided to deliver His commandments, laws, and statutes to humanity through Moses, to orient and guide us in God's way while the Messiah had not yet come (Exodus 24:12). Nevertheless, we did not change for the better. On the contrary, we grew worse and worse, and the Law became shackles that imprisoned us and condemned us to inevitable death (Romans 3:23 and 6:23). God, knowing our situation and being a merciful Father, had His plan for our salvation already in motion. He had already promised Abraham that all would be saved through his offspring (Genesis 12:3). This offspring that God promised would come into the world — and whom all the prophets announced as the Messiah — was Jesus Christ. It was only when Jesus came into the world that we obtained the possibility of being freed from the prison of the Law, provided we accept Him as our Lord and Saviour (John 3:16). Those who accepted Him were sealed with the seal of God's Spirit, so that our hearts might change and we might become more like Jesus (Ephesians 1:13–14). In this way we are adopted as children of God, brothers and sisters of Jesus, and fellow citizens of the world to come with Christ (Galatians 3:29). Through Him, we will also be able to overcome death through the resurrection and receive eternal life.
2. **Unity in Christ.** The other consequence of the coming of Christ was the union of all nations, tribes, and peoples into one single people — the people of God. Before His arrival, we were constantly divided in every sphere (e.g., tribal, racial, spiritual, social, territorial, etc.). These differences throughout history were the cause of great tension, enmity, and war. When Jesus came into the world, He broke all these taboos and destroyed the wall of enmity that divided humanity (Ephesians 2:14–18). For despite all our differences — and they are many — we have something in common: we are all members of the same family, since we were all adopted by God through Jesus Christ. This reality is extremely crucial for the human rights of every citizen in the world. It was thanks to Jesus that, in countries with Christian roots, ideas such as there is neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male nor female, for we are all one in Christ influenced politics. Consequently, many of the advances in human rights throughout history were achieved thanks to Christianity — that is, thanks to Jesus Christ — because He united us all in His body. Therefore, we all return to being equal, and no one is superior to their neighbour — regardless of wealth, social class, power, or anything else.
