# Devotional 1 Timothy 6: The Love of Money and True Wealth

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Timothy, chapter 6, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul concludes his first letter to his son in faith, Timothy, explaining how the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil, and that Timothy must flee from all of that and focus on remaining a faithful servant of God. Finally, Paul instructs him to command the rich not to be arrogant and not to set their hope on wealth.

## Observations

1. **The love of money is the root of all kinds of evil.** Greed for money plunges people into ruin and destruction. This happens because those who want to get rich quickly end up falling into temptation and becoming slaves to many desires (1 Timothy 6:9–10). Desires that seek to satisfy the pleasures of the flesh rather than the pleasures of the Spirit. Consequently, by yielding to temptation and feeding these carnal desires, they begin to drift away from the faith. For the flesh desires what is contrary to the Spirit. Therefore, there is no way to live by the flesh and at the same time live by the Spirit, since the two oppose each other and cannot coexist (Galatians 5:16–17). Unfortunately, there are many people who see the church as a business plan and seek the pulpit to enrich themselves at the expense of others' genuine faith (1 Timothy 6:3–5). This religious hypocrisy is one of the greatest reasons for non-believers being alienated from God. That is why Paul said that God's name is blasphemed among unbelievers because of spiritual fraudsters (Romans 2:17–24). People like that who cause others to stumble, abandon the faith, or never believe in God because of them will face the harshest punishment of all (Luke 17:1–2). Additionally, Paul also instructs Timothy to warn the rich not to be arrogant and not to set their hope on wealth. For this is a fleeting wealth that you cannot take with you into the world to come. What they should truly seek to be rich in is good works, generosity, and willingness to help others. For in doing so they will store up treasure for the future and obtain eternal life (1 Timothy 6:17–19).
