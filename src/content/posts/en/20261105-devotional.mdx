# Devotional Hebrews 4: Entering God's Rest and Jesus Our High Priest

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining two crucial truths. First, the author explains that as believers in Jesus Christ, we must be careful not to fall short, just as the first ones who heard the good news did (that is, the wilderness generation whom Moses led toward the promised land and who disobeyed God). Second, the author explains that as Christians, we have a High Priest in Jesus Christ whom we can fully trust. For He sympathizes with our weaknesses, having lived as a human being just like us, yet without sin.

## Observations

1. **As Christians, we must take care not to lose our salvation like the first generation that heard the good news.** The author's observation refers to the generation in the wilderness whom God brought out of Egypt through Moses, who continually refused to obey Him, even when they were on the threshold of entering the promised land. God punished them on account of their unbelief, rebellion, and disobedience (Numbers 14:22–23, 29–30; Psalm 95:11). We too, like that wilderness generation, have heard the gospel and share the hope of entering God's rest. However, that lost generation heard the good news and yet it profited them nothing, because they persisted in disobedience and acted directly contrary to what God commanded. This serves as a warning for us not to repeat their mistakes and forfeit our salvation (Hebrews 4:1–2). Therefore, our calling is to do our part so that as many people as possible may enter this rest with God, preparing for Jesus to return in all His glory in the last days (Matthew 24:14). That is to say, we must seek to cultivate the fruit and gifts of the Spirit, living as faithful servants of God worthy to be called His children. Furthermore, we must proclaim the gospel to every creature in all the world until the end comes. These are the spiritual safeguards we must follow to remain in salvation and avoid losing what has been promised.
