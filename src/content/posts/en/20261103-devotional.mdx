# Devotional Hebrews 2: Why Jesus Became Human Like Us

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 2, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining that Jesus Christ was made like His brothers in every way (that is, like us). For He came as a human being and not as a spirit, so that He might experience the same temptations as we do. And through all the suffering He endured, He was made perfect and fulfilled the sacrifice to rescue all who believe in Him from death and the clutches of the Devil.

## Observations

1. **Jesus came as a human being just like us to be a faithful and merciful high priest in service to God, in order to make atonement for the sins of the people.** These are two of the reasons for the coming of Jesus Christ that the author of the letter listed. However, there are three additional reasons why God the Son chose to come to earth as a human being named Jesus of Nazareth. The first was to reveal His true character to us. That is, God the Son could have come in any form He desired, but He chose ours in order to demonstrate His true character in a way with which we could identify and relate more closely. In the Old Testament, when God revealed Himself in various other forms (such as a voice, a burning bush, light, etc.), we could not relate as well to Him because we were consumed by fear. In other words, we were unable to discern His true character clearly because of the manner in which He appeared and because fear overtook us (Exodus 3:2, 13:21, 24:9–11, 40:34–35; Genesis 18:1–2; and 1 Kings 19:11–12). But because Jesus was a human being, it became easier to perceive the true character of God from a human perspective. The second was to relate directly to us. That is to say, God is not an indifferent deity unconcerned with what happens to us or what we do, as deists (those who believe God created the universe but does not care about what happens or intervene actively) claim. On the contrary, by taking on human nature in the person of Jesus Christ, God demonstrates that He loves us, desires the very best for us, and therefore came to save us from our sins (John 3:16). Through Jesus, we also discover that God is compassionate, patient, gentle, good, faithful, humble, and self-controlled (Galatians 5:22–23). It was through Jesus Christ that we can undoubtedly witness all these attributes of God in the person who faithfully mirrors His nature. The third was to humble Himself and serve us. That is, to give the greatest example of leadership in human history. God, who is omnipotent, omniscient, and omnipresent, took on human flesh in Jesus to serve us. In other words, God humbled Himself by becoming a human being (limiting His powers), to dwell among us, serve us, fulfill the entire Law, and then be crucified to save us from our sins. This is the supreme model of leadership, for Jesus never instructed us to do anything that He did not first demonstrate Himself. That is why He declared twice that a servant is not greater than his master (John 13:16, 15:20). The first time He said this was while serving His disciples by washing each of their feet, setting an example that no one should ever consider themselves above serving others, regardless of their position or status. And the second time He said this was to warn those same disciples that they would also face persecution just as He was persecuted for preaching the gospel. Therefore, Jesus came to reveal many mysteries concerning God and His plan for our salvation.
