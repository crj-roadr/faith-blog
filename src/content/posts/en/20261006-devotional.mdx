# Devotional Philippians 2: Be the Light That Shines in the Darkness

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philippians, chapter 2, verses 12–18, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all the believers in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, exhorting them to continue obeying and keeping Christ's commandments without complaints even in his absence until the day of Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **We must be the light that illuminates a world of darkness and shadows.** This is a commandment that Jesus left us and that Paul reiterates in these verses. When Jesus said in His Sermon on the Mount that Christians are the light of the world, He explained that light was made to shine for everyone. Therefore, our good deeds before men serve to illuminate them and show them what is possible when one believes in Christ. And consequently, these same good deeds will serve to glorify our Father in heaven (Matthew 5:14-16). For others will know that we have the ability to do these good things that others cannot achieve precisely because of God. Furthermore, this characteristic that light has of illuminating and guiding people in dark places is directly related to leadership. That is, one cannot be a light without having the characteristics of leadership. One of these characteristics is setting the example and taking the initiative to do and preach what is right in God's eyes even when it is not reciprocated or the other person would not do the same. For example, seeking to forgive our neighbor when they hurt us instead of holding grudges or seeking revenge. Especially because Jesus told the apostle Peter, when he asked how many times he should forgive a brother who sins against him, that we must forgive 70 times 7—that is, always (Matthew 18:21–22). After all, we sin against God every day and ask for forgiveness hoping that He in His infinite mercy will forgive us. Therefore, we cannot be hypocrites and say that our brother can only sin against me a couple of times and on the third I will not forgive him. For the same criteria we use to judge others will be used to judge us as well (Matthew 7:2). That is why loving our neighbor and being able to forgive them as many times as necessary is the correct and worthy attitude of a being of light (i.e., a Christian). And the same applies to all the other fruits of the Spirit. We must be joyful in the face of afflictions, be patient with others, be peacemakers and resolve conflicts, be kind to others, be faithful to God and to our neighbor, be humble in life, and have self-control (Galatians 5:22–23). To honor Jesus by fulfilling His word about the Christian being the light of the world, living by the Spirit is the necessary conduct to achieve it.
