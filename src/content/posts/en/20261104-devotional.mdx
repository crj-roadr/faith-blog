# Devotional Hebrews 3: Jesus Is Greater than Moses and the Warning

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining two crucial truths. First, the author explains that Jesus Christ is greater than Moses, for Moses was a faithful servant in all of God's house, whereas Jesus was faithful as a Son over God's house. Second, the author warns all who have heard the gospel of God and the voice of the Holy Spirit not to continue in a sinful, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God.

## Observations

1. **Jesus Christ is worthy of greater honor than Moses, just as the builder of a house receives greater honor than the house itself.** The author made this observation so that his target audience (Jewish believers) would understand the true magnitude and importance of Jesus. In Jewish tradition, Moses is regarded as the greatest and most significant prophet of all time. In contrast, they did not consider Jesus Christ to be a prophet, nor the Messiah, let alone divine. Therefore, the author had to demonstrate how Jesus Christ is the seed of Abraham through whom all humanity is blessed and saved, exactly as Scripture foretold (Genesis 12:3; 22:18). Thus, Jesus Christ is the Messiah and the Prophet whom the Jewish people had long awaited. Furthermore, He is the prophesied Son of Man and, consequently, the Son of God—that is, deity incarnate (Daniel 7:13–14). For this reason, the author compared Jesus to Moses and argued that Christ is worthy of greater honor than Moses because He is the builder of God's house, while Moses was merely a faithful servant in all God's house (Hebrews 3:3–6). In other words, it was through Jesus that all things were created (John 1:1–3).
2. **We must take care not to harden our hearts and ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit, lest we forfeit our salvation.** The author also gives a solemn warning against this sin because, according to Jesus Christ, it is the only sin that will not be forgiven (Matthew 12:31–32; Mark 3:28–29; Luke 12:10). The reason this specific sin is unforgivable—neither in this age nor in the age to come—is that the person persistently refuses to listen to the voice of God's Spirit. That is, whoever commits this sin adamantly refuses to repent of their sins; each time they do so, their heart grows harder until they can no longer hear the voice of the Holy Spirit at all (Matthew 13:15). This is the worst state into which a person can fall. For it is through the Holy Spirit that we receive spiritual rebirth and renewal, as well as the gifts and fruit of the Spirit, enabling us to walk faithfully in God's ways (Ezekiel 36:26–27). And to receive the Holy Spirit, we must believe and accept Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior (Ephesians 1:13–14; Acts 2:38; Galatians 3:14). Without receiving and heeding the voice of the Spirit of God, we can never be delivered from death and hell. That is why persistent unbelief causes people to forfeit the grace of salvation that Christ offers.
