# Devotional Galatians 3: Faith, the Law, and God's Promise

Today's devotional is found in the book of Galatians, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Galatia, warning them that we are justified by faith and not by the Law. Furthermore, Paul also explains the relationship and purpose of the Law in light of the promise God made to Abraham. Finally, Paul explains that we are all children of God through faith in Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **Christians are justified by faith.** Paul warns the Galatians against distorting the gospel of Christ when they look to the Law as the vehicle of their salvation. He questions them whether they received the Holy Spirit by the works demanded by the Law or by faith upon accepting Jesus Christ as their Lord and Savior (Galatians 3:2). Paul explains to them that we are not justified by the Law, but by faith. For even Abraham was justified by his faith in God and not by observing the Law (Galatians 3:6). Therefore, all those who identify as children of Abraham are those who live by faith. Indeed, the Scriptures foresaw that God would justify the nations by faith, which is why He announced the gospel to Abraham beforehand. For in the book of Genesis, chapter 12, God promises Abraham that all peoples on earth will be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). And the same is confirmed in the gospel when Jesus said that all who accept Him as Lord and Savior shall not perish but have eternal life (John 3:16). God did this to redeem us from the curse of the Law, since no one would be able to keep the law except Jesus Christ. Therefore, whoever lives by the Law is already condemned to death, because they cannot keep it. Thus, our only salvation is through faith in Christ, because He was the only one capable of fulfilling the Law for us and thereby saving us from the curse.
2. **The purpose of the Law in light of the promise God made to Abraham.** I believe many might be asking: why then did God give us the Law? The answer is very simple. God decided to give us the Law so that it would serve as a guide and tutor to God's Will before the arrival of Jesus. Because on our own we cannot follow God's way and always end up sinning and bringing more and more condemnation upon the world. That is, as we continue to sin, sin continues to spread and corrupt the world (people and all of creation). In other words, the Law never came to annul the promise God made to Abraham. On the contrary, it was introduced to guide us until the promise was fulfilled, much like a substitute teacher who teaches in the absence of the head teacher. Furthermore, when God makes the promise to Abraham referring to his seed (and not his seeds), He is referring to a single person, Jesus Christ (Galatians 3:16). That is, in Genesis, God was already proclaiming the gospel of Christ as the Lord and Savior of all nations of the world.
3. **We are all children of God through our faith in Christ.** For all who are baptized into Christ and accept Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior are automatically clothed with Christ. That is, as Christians our lives belong to Jesus. Moreover, by belonging to Christ, we are also Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise of God (Galatians 3:26–29). It is precisely for this reason that Jesus is our only salvation, for He is the way, the truth, and the life (John 14:6). When we piece together all these passages, all the pieces of the puzzle fit together perfectly. Furthermore, all this also reveals to us that God had everything planned from the very beginning.
