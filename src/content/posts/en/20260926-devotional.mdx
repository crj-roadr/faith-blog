# Devotional Galatians 5: Freedom in Christ and the Fruit of the Spirit

Today's devotional is found in the book of Galatians, chapter 5, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Galatia, explaining to them the freedom we have in Christ now that we are no longer under the Law. However, he also warns them not to abuse this freedom to surrender to their own passions. Finally, Paul explains that now that we believe in Jesus, we must live by the Spirit and not by the desires of the flesh, for the two are opposed and cannot coexist.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **Freedom in Christ.** Paul explains that thanks to Christ, we were set free from slavery under the Law. We previously had to obey numerous laws that we could rarely fulfill. Because of this, we were held captive when we lived under the Law prior to Christ. All of our natural inclinations led us into sin precisely because of our sinful nature (Ecclesiastes 7:20 and Romans 3:10). Consequently, God had to give us many laws so that we could guide ourselves and begin walking in His way (Galatians 3:19, 24). Yet doing so proved impossible for us. Only when Jesus came to sacrifice Himself for us by fulfilling the whole Law were we set free from the slavery of the Law (Galatians 2:16, 3:13, and 4:4–5). We no longer need circumcision, sacrifices for the atonement of sins, and many other rituals. In other words, we finally obtained greater freedom to live life with the guarantee of eternal life, provided we believe in Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior (John 3:16). But what Paul clarifies in this letter is that this does not grant us license to do whatever we feel like doing (Galatians 5:13–14). On the contrary, having greater freedom means we must be vigilant not to fall into sin. Furthermore, we must act responsibly and recognize that not everyone possesses enough spiritual maturity to discern what is permissible and in what measure. Therefore, we must abstain from doing things that could become stumbling blocks for others (Matthew 18:7).
2. **Life by the Holy Spirit.** This is what we as Christians must now prioritize, leaving the life of the flesh behind. According to Paul, the desires of the flesh are completely different from the desires of the Spirit. Because of this, they cannot coexist in harmony; rather, they stand in direct opposition. That is, when we indulge the desires of the flesh, the spirit is weakened while the flesh and its desires are strengthened (and vice versa). Therefore, there is no way to strike a balance between the two, because such a compromise does not exist (Galatians 5:16–17). Thus, we must be careful, watchful, and alert to discern whether we are satisfying the desires of the flesh or the desires of the Spirit. Paul lists the following as works of the flesh that we must turn away from: sexual immorality, impurity, and debauchery; idolatry and witchcraft; hatred, discord, jealousy, fits of rage, selfish ambition, dissensions, factions, and envy; drunkenness, orgies, and the like. He also adds that those who live like this will not inherit the kingdom of God (Galatians 5:19–21). In contrast, the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law. Therefore, those who belong to Christ must crucify the flesh with its passions and desires (Galatians 5:22–24).
