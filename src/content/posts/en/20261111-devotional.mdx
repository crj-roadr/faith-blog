# Devotional Hebrews 9:11-28: The Perfect Sacrifice and the Blood of Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 9, verses 11–28, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining to them why Jesus entered the heavenly sanctuary as High Priest through the blood He shed for the sins of all those who believe in Him as their Lord and Savior.

## Observations

1. **The blood of Jesus Christ was used to atone for our sins.** The author explains in these verses how Jesus Christ ultimately became the mediator of the new covenant with God. Jesus enters the true sanctuary, found in heaven, as High Priest with His own blood to atone for all the sins of those who believe in Him as Lord and Savior. Just as the high priests did with the blood of goats or calves every time they had to expiate their own sins or those of the people committed in ignorance. The difference is that Christ needed to sacrifice Himself only once because His blood was pure enough to cleanse all of us. In reality, Jesus came into the world as a testator to write His will, which would only come into effect upon His death. In this will, Christ included all believers who placed their faith in God and, consequently, in Him. That is why all believers are co-heirs of the promised inheritance—that is, eternal life (John 3:16). We can also observe in these passages that no covenant can be established with God without blood. For even the first covenant that God established with the people of Israel through Moses was ratified with the blood of calves (Hebrews 9:18–20). For the wages of sin is always death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 6:23). That is why Jesus had to come and shed His blood once for us to pay the deadly price of our sins in our place. Therefore, we have a choice to make: either we receive eternal life by accepting Christ as our Lord and Savior, or we are condemned to death and hell by rejecting Him.
