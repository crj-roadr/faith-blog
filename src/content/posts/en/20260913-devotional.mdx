# Devotional 2 Corinthians 5: Heavenly Dwelling and Reconciliation

Today's devotional is found in the book of 2 Corinthians, chapter 5, and this was what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his second letter to the church of Corinth, explaining that as Christians we have steadfast hope even while living in a temporary earthly tent, because we know we have an eternal heavenly dwelling. Furthermore, he explains how we have inherited the ministry of reconciliation with God through the gospel of Jesus Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Our hope as Christians stems from the conviction that we possess an eternal dwelling in heaven with Christ, even though we presently live in a temporary tent.** This is the profound difference between believers and non-believers. Life in this fallen world is not easy. Jesus Himself reminded us that in this world we will face tribulation. Indeed, every human being shares the universal experience of adversity, suffering, and sorrow. Consequently, many people find themselves embittered, depressed, or carrying an overwhelming emptiness in their hearts. The remedies the world offers to fill that void—wealth, physical beauty, fame, status, or power—inevitably fail. In reality, pursuing them does the exact opposite, enslaving people to the very idols they worship. Worldly pursuits can never satisfy a spiritual longing that only God can fill. That is the tragic dilemma facing those without Christ. Yet for us who believe, we have the true and lasting answer: God Himself. He is the almighty, omnipresent, and omniscient Creator, characterized by absolute perfection, love, and righteousness (Luke 1:37, Psalm 139:7–10, and Proverbs 15:3). Moreover, He is a personal God with whom we, as relational beings, can walk in intimate fellowship (Exodus 33:11). He truly loves us and cares deeply about our condition. He cared so much that He initiated the divine plan of reconciliation to rescue us: sending His one and only Son so that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life (John 3:16). That is why Jesus urged us to take heart amid our afflictions, for He has overcome the world (John 16:33).
2. **We have inherited the ministry of reconciliation with God through the gospel of Jesus Christ.** Christ has entrusted us with the sacred responsibility of spreading His gospel so that others may be saved and receive eternal life just as we have (Matthew 28:19–20 and John 3:16–17). This is a solemn calling that demands our utmost commitment. Jesus taught that the greatest commandments are to love God with all our heart, soul, and mind, and to love our neighbor as ourselves (Matthew 22:37–40). A person cannot genuinely claim to love their neighbor while possessing the cure to a fatal disease and refusing to share it (Matthew 5:43–44). Keeping the message of salvation to ourselves is selfish and unworthy of anyone who bears the name of Christ. When we witness suffering, despair, grief, injustice, and malice in this world, we know instinctively that humanity was not created to live this way. Our spirits rightly mourn over the brokenness and cruelty of the world (Matthew 5:6), because deep within our souls we know we were made by a loving, just, and merciful God who desires our ultimate good. This underscores the vital necessity of proclaiming Christ's gospel to everyone. People desperately need to discover their true identity, divine purpose, and eternal salvation. When someone surrenders their life to God, they receive living hope, supernatural peace, divine comfort, unwavering faith, and spiritual discipline. They become equipped to stand firm amidst life's fiercest storms. Therefore, if we desire to honor Jesus Christ and our Heavenly Father, we must love our neighbors by guiding them toward the salvation freely offered in the gospel.
