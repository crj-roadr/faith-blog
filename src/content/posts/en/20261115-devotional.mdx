# Devotional James 2: Why Faith Without Works Is Dead

Today's devotional is found in the book of James, chapter 2, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, James writes a letter addressed to the Christians of the twelve tribes of Israel scattered throughout the world, explaining to them why favoritism among us is forbidden to Christians and why faith by itself without works is dead.

## Observations

1. **Favoritism regarding treating some favorably and others unfavorably is forbidden to Christians.** This observation is one of the most vital. For it is directly linked to the second greatest commandment according to Jesus Christ, which is to love our neighbor as ourselves (Matthew 22:37–40). Yet, unfortunately, this is a problem we observe every day, even among Christians. We see church pastors who treat members with greater purchasing power better than members with less. We see pastors who prefer shepherding churches with members of high socioeconomic status and look down upon other churches with economically disadvantaged members. But this also happens among all of us, not just pastors. Often, we show favoritism without even realizing it. When we treat someone we love with kindness and someone we dislike with harshness, that is practicing favoritism. What we must understand is that God has no favorites, for we are all God's creation. We were all created in His image and likeness. Therefore, everyone is worthy of being treated with love, dignity, respect, and grace. After all, no one enjoys being treated with contempt. Thus, we must never do the same to our neighbor, whoever they may be.
2. **Faith by itself, without works, is dead.** This is a warning that James gives directly to Christians of Jewish background, but it applies to all Christians universally. That is, one cannot simply have faith in God without the deeds that demonstrate that faith. Just as we cannot claim to believe in mutual respect while slandering our neighbor; or claim to believe in freedom of expression while seeking to silence those whose opinions we dislike; or claim to love our partners when times are good, but when separating seek to inflict suffering and maximum harm upon our exes. This is hypocrisy, and our behavior reveals what we truly believe. For however hard it is to admit, whenever our professed beliefs and our actions do not align, it is our actions that reveal what we genuinely believe. This is why Jesus told us that we will know people by their fruit: a good tree always produces good fruit, and a bad tree always produces bad fruit—a good tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a bad tree bear good fruit (Matthew 7:17–20). Furthermore, this is only what we as human beings can perceive through outward actions; now imagine God, who knows all the intimate thoughts of our hearts. Therefore, when we claim to believe in God while our actions contradict our beliefs, we are merely deceiving ourselves (never God). For the day will come when many will call upon Jesus' name, claiming they prophesied in His name, drove out demons, and performed miracles, yet they will be cast out because Jesus never knew them—that is, they never kept or lived out His commandments (Matthew 7:21–23). Therefore, we must be exceedingly careful not to play Russian roulette with our salvation.
