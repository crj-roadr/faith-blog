# Devotional 1 Timothy 1: False Doctrines and God's Forgiveness

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Timothy, chapter 1, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes his first letter to his spiritual son in the faith, Timothy, instructing him to command certain false teachers of the law to stop teaching misleading doctrines that distort the gospel. In addition, Paul gives heartfelt thanks to Jesus Christ for the grace and mercy poured out upon him, even when he considered himself the foremost of sinners.

## Observations

1. **We must beware of false teachers of the law.** For they are not focused on doing the will of God or proclaiming the gospel of Christ with sound doctrine. Instead, they are more preoccupied with debating topics that do nothing to advance God's purpose, such as religious legalism, endless genealogies, and speculative myths that have nothing to do with being a faithful disciple of Christ (1 Timothy 1:3–4). Moreover, such teachings confuse people and ultimately drive them further away from God. They cause immense harm to believers and unbelievers alike, stirring up useless controversies, creating division among brothers and sisters, and placing stumbling blocks in the path of new believers. This is one of the most perilous things one can do. It is not by chance that Jesus warned that it would be better to have a millstone tied around one's neck and be thrown into the sea than to cause one of these little ones who believe in Him to stumble (Luke 17:1–2). Therefore, we must remain vigilant and grounded in the word of wisdom and knowledge, capable of discerning sound doctrine from falsehood. It is only when we truly know the gospel of Christ and the Word of God that we can walk the path of holiness, recognize stumbling blocks, and steer clear of them.
2. **We must believe that anyone can be saved by Christ, regardless of their past sins.** Because God is almighty, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast mercy (Exodus 34:6). That is to say, no person has fallen so deep into sin that God lacks the power to redeem and transform them. Believing otherwise is a cunning trap of the enemy designed to drive us further from God through paralyzing guilt. That is why we must be very careful with toxic guilt. Satan uses it to harden our hearts so that we refuse to forgive ourselves, even when God has already pardoned us. This selfish guilt does not stem from genuine humility; it comes from pride. It presumes that our standard of judgment is higher than God's—reasoning that while God might forgive us, we cannot forgive ourselves for what we have done. That is sheer arrogance, not humility. We must discern the difference so we do not fall prey to this snare. Furthermore, we must never judge others, deeming someone more sinful than ourselves or assuming that anyone is beyond the reach of God's forgiveness. That, too, is spiritual arrogance and religious pride. This was precisely the error Jonah committed when he resisted God's will and refused to preach to Nineveh. Jonah knew that even though the Assyrians were ruthless enemies of Israel who committed unspeakable cruelties (such as skinning captives alive, mass impalements, decapitations, and brutal mass deportations), God would still show mercy and forgive them if they genuinely repented of their wickedness (Jonah 4:1–3). Now, the only sin that remains unforgivable is blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (Matthew 12:31–32). That refers to the person who stubbornly refuses to repent, profanes the Spirit of grace, blames God for all the evil in the world, harbors bitter resentment against the Lord, and permanently hardens their heart against God's conviction. That is the state Jesus warned the Pharisees about. For this reason, regardless of the gravity of the sins a person has committed, God will readily forgive them as long as they do not harden their heart and blaspheme against His Holy Spirit.
