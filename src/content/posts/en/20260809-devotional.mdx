# Devotional Romans 2: God's Righteous Judgment and Hypocrisy

Today's devotional is found in the book of Romans, chapter 2, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul wrote another letter to warn and alert the Romans (Jews and non-Jews) about God's righteous judgment and the danger of Jewish hypocrisy.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **God's righteous judgment.** Paul warns and alerts the Romans who believe they have the right to judge others for the sins they commit, as if they themselves were innocent of sin (the very same sins they judge in others). Unfortunately, we all deal with this both from the perspective of the receiver and the giver. We all feel that sense of rejection, contempt, and judgment when someone judges us. Thus, we all know that it is wrong to judge others for the sins they commit because we do not like being judged. Furthermore, there is no one on the face of the earth who is good in God's eyes; God alone is good (Mark 10:18). In other words, we are all sinners. Moreover, the consequences of judging others do not remain only in this world. The same measuring stick we use to judge others will be the same one God uses with us (Matthew 7:1–2), for God shows no favoritism (Romans 2:11). Therefore, Paul alerts us that judging others—even internally—is useless. God is righteous and omniscient; He judges according to the truth (Romans 2:2). And the truth is that we judge others for the same sins we also commit, and God will punish us for that as well (Romans 2:1). For this reason, we must thank God, abandon stubbornness, and repent of our sins. He will give eternal life to all who persevere in faith and good works, seeking His glory (Romans 2:7). But those who self-seekingly reject the truth will suffer God's wrath and anger (Romans 2:8).
2. **Christian hypocrisy drives non-Christians away from God.** This is a reality we all know. I myself, in my walk of faith in God, also witnessed this hypocrisy. At that time, I was still a child and lacked spiritual maturity and knowledge of God's Word as I have now. Thus, this observation is personal to me. It was precisely this incongruity between the beliefs and actions of Christians in the church that led me to run away from there. Because of this, Paul calls out the Jews who received the Law of God and believe they have the discernment to know right from wrong. The very ones who claim to be guides to the blind and a light to those in darkness commit the same sins they condemn in others (Romans 2:17–23). Paul reminds them that because of them, non-Jews blaspheme God's name and turn away from Him (Romans 2:24). This is the same warning we must heed as Christians. When we proclaim to the world that we are Christians, our attitudes and actions affect how non-Christians view God, Jesus Christ, and Holy Scripture. This is because actions speak louder about what we believe than words do. That is why Jesus said you would know the tree by its fruit: a good tree produces good fruit, and a bad tree produces bad fruit (Matthew 7:16–20). However, with greater spiritual maturity and knowledge of the Word, I now know that we are all hypocrites to some extent, as we all continue to sin every day. The church is the place for everyone who needs forgiveness and salvation through Jesus Christ. Jesus answered the Pharisees by stating that the healthy do not need a doctor, but the sick do; He came to call sinners, not the righteous (Mark 2:17). Therefore, we must understand and show compassion toward our fellow Christians, for we are all sinners and hypocrites. We should not condemn them for being sinners (just like us), because that is precisely why Jesus came and sacrificed Himself—to save us from our sins and grant us eternal life.
