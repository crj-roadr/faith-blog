# Devotional 2 Thessalonians 2: The Man of Lawlessness and the Victory of Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of 2 Thessalonians, chapter 2, verses 1–12, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul explains in his second letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Thessalonica, that they should not be alarmed by the manifestations of the man of lawlessness who will come by the activity of Satan, with all power and false signs and wonders. For this prophecy must come to pass first so that Jesus may come to destroy him, along with Satan and all evil.

## Observations

1. **First comes the man of lawlessness, only to be destroyed later by Jesus Christ.** Unfortunately, this is the reality we live in today because of humanity's disobedience to God from the very beginning. Ever since Adam and Eve sinned against God by eating the fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, sin and death entered into the world (Genesis 3:11–19). Consequently, we were cast out of the Garden of Eden for our disobedience and for presuming to be the arbiters of morality (Genesis 3:22–23). Because of our actions, the world was cursed and has fallen increasingly under the influence of Satan. This disobedience corrupted our good nature and made us slaves to sin. It is for this reason that our nature today is sinful rather than holy. And that is why it is so difficult for us to follow God's will and choose to live a life of holiness (Romans 7:18, 8:7–8). Because the desires of the flesh oppose the desires of the Spirit. They are incompatible. The more we live according to one, the harder it becomes to live according to the other (Galatians 5:16–17). This condition led us to be condemned to death for having sinned against God (Romans 5:12). For God is a perfect Being, and when He created the universe, He made it perfect (Genesis 1:31, Matthew 5:48, and Luke 18:19). Yet because of our sins, death entered creation, and all creation was subjected to a curse. Moreover, since the wages of sin is death and we are guilty of bringing death into the world, we stand condemned to death (Romans 6:23). Nevertheless, God, knowing our plight and desiring to save us from this tragic end, in His infinite mercy, love, and grace, conceived a plan of redemption to deliver us from death and the hell awaiting us. God decided to send His only begotten Son so that everyone who believes in Him should not perish but have eternal life (John 3:16). Therefore, Jesus came and lived a sinless, perfect life to offer the perfect sacrifice required to deliver us from the yoke of the Law that condemned us to death (Hebrews 9:12, 10:14). Before the coming of Jesus, we were under God's Law and could only be justified if we kept it completely. But now, all who believe in Christ are under God's grace and are justified by faith, not by works (Romans 3:23–24 and Ephesians 2:8–9). When we accept Christ as our Lord and Savior, we also accept His sacrifice on our behalf. And because Christ fulfilled the entire Law in our place and paid the penalty for us, He freed us from death. However, if anyone rejects Jesus' sacrifice and does not accept Him as Lord and Savior, they remain under the Law and will consequently be condemned to death and cast into hell for all eternity (Galatians 5:3–4 and John 3:18). When Jesus rose from the dead, and before ascending to God the Father, He promised that He would return a second time to judge the world and destroy evil and Satan once and for all (John 14:3 and Matthew 13:41–42). Furthermore, He warned us that an era would come marked by rampant evil, but that all these things must take place to fulfill prophecy before the final judgment (Matthew 24:4–13). That is why Paul instructed the Thessalonians not to be alarmed when people arise claiming to come from God while performing false signs, wonders, and miracles, because these are deceptive and do not come from God (2 Thessalonians 2:1–3, 8–9). Even so, this man of lawlessness must first be revealed so that ultimately all evil and Satan himself may be brought to an end.
