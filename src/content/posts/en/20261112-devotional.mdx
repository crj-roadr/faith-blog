# Devotional Hebrews 11: True Faith and Salvation by Grace

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 11, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining to them why all their ancestors were saved by faith in God.

## Observations

1. **Faith is being confident in what we hope for and being certain of what we do not see.** This observation by the author is one of the best descriptions of what faith is. The only detail I would add to this definition is that faith is based on evidence of trustworthiness and credibility. That is, we can be confident in what we hope for and certain of what we do not see because we know that the entity in whom we place our faith is reliable and credible — for it has already demonstrated its capacity to fulfill its promises in the past. As Christians, faith is our refuge and the means by which we will obtain salvation, just as all believers in God have from the beginning of creation. There is a popular interpretation of Holy Scripture that claims God changed His plans — that in the Old Testament He was stricter and people were justified by keeping the Law. But this interpretation is mistaken. For from the moment humanity fell into sin, God always intended to justify us through faith (Genesis 3:11–19; Romans 3:28, 5:1). We see these examples throughout the Old Testament itself. When God told Noah to take his family, two animals of each species (a male and a female), and food into the ark that God commanded him to build to protect them from the flood, Noah had faith in God and did exactly what God commanded (Genesis 6:13–22). When God commanded Abraham to leave his homeland and go with his family toward the promised land that God would give to his descendants — who would be innumerable and a blessing to all nations — Abraham obeyed and left everything to follow God (Genesis 12:1–4). Furthermore, when God spoke with Moses through the burning bush and commanded him to follow His instructions because He would bring His people (the Israelites) out of Egypt to lead them to the promised land, Moses — even fearing the consequences from Pharaoh — obeyed and trusted in God (Exodus 3:1–12). These are just a few of the many examples found in the Old Testament of believers in God being saved by their faith. That is why, when Paul declared that we will be saved from hell by the grace of God through our faith, he was simply putting into words what had already been happening throughout the Old Testament, and he confirmed that the promise of the Messiah fulfilled by Jesus was the same promise God had made to Abraham (Ephesians 2:8–9; Galatians 2:16). In other words, our entire walk with God and our subsequent salvation depend on our faith in Him — and this has always been so from the very beginning, even before original sin.
