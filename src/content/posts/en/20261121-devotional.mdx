# 1 Peter 3 Devotional: Marital Duties and Suffering for Doing Good

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Peter, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Peter writes a letter addressed to Christians scattered throughout the world, commanding them to fulfill their marital duties and to do good even if they suffer.

## Observations

1. **Wives, submit to your husbands so that if your husbands do not believe in the word, they may be won over more by your respectful conduct than by your words.** This is yet another observation that sounds very radical to us. In the world we live in, this kind of behavior is not recommended. In our society, what is recommended is the law of reciprocity. That is, if someone treats you well, you treat them well in return. But if someone mistreats me, I will mistreat them back. In other words, the famous eye for an eye and tooth for a tooth. So when we recommend this conduct to women today, to them it seems foolish. And in a certain way, they are right—if our belief is that the world's wisdom is correct relative to God's wisdom. However, as Christians, no matter how much our experience as sinners and former defenders of worldly wisdom instructs us toward reciprocity, we must follow God. Therefore, all women who believe this is not right should look at Jesus Christ, the one we claim to follow. For Jesus Christ was very clear when He said that the servant is never greater than his master, for He came to serve and we must follow His example (John 13:16). And if we truly love Him as we profess, then we will keep His commandments (John 14:15). Furthermore, we must not forget that it is God's will that we organize our homes according to His divine order. That is, the head of the woman is the man, the head of the man is Christ, and the head of Christ is God the Father (1 Corinthians 11:3). This is the perfect order for things to go well according to God. Therefore, if we believe in God as we have said, we must obey Him.
2. **Husbands must be understanding in their married lives and respect their wives, since women are more delicate and co-heirs of the inheritance.** This means that husbands must not abuse their position and mistreat their wives. It is entirely the opposite. Husbands must love their wives as Christ loved the church and was willing to sacrifice Himself for her (Ephesians 5:25). For husbands have the obligation to lead and set an example so that the people who depend on them can follow. That is why Paul said that both must submit to God first and then fulfill their marital duties (Ephesians 5:21 and 1 Corinthians 7:3). In other words, the fact that the husband is the head of his household does not mean he should not serve his wife and children. He must set the example and be the first to serve them, just as Jesus Christ demonstrated when He washed the feet of His disciples to teach them that everyone must serve one another. For the servant is not greater than his master. That is, if Jesus is serving His disciples and people who should worship Him, then all His followers must serve their neighbor as well (John 13:16).
3. **It is better to suffer for doing good than for doing evil.** This is an observation that the world rejects. For according to earthly wisdom, it is better to suffer doing evil than to suffer for doing good. That is why many end up justifying not doing what is right, succumbing to the desires of the flesh, and following the counsel of the crowd. Because, according to them, they end up being more respected by others and can do whatever they please. This is the true motive behind this way of thinking—wanting to live life according to one's own beliefs and desires. They simply use the excuse that it is not worth being good because you gain nothing by being a good person. But they deceive themselves when they think this way, for they are simply considering their lives here on earth as if it were the final destination. In reality, we have everything to lose, for we lose salvation and eternal life in the presence of God, Jesus Christ, the saints, and our loved ones. The fact that we do not believe an eternity exists that we will have to live after our death does not mean it is not true. Therefore, playing Russian roulette with our lives is the most foolish thing we can do. For all the things we value here on earth that we want to achieve and the riches we want to accumulate will be of no use for our salvation. For no one can take the riches they accumulate on earth (Matthew 6:19–21). Therefore, it is indeed better to suffer being a good person than to suffer being a bad person, for blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God (Matthew 5:8).
