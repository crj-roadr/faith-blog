# Devotional Galatians 2: Justified by Faith, Not by the Law

Today's devotional is found in the book of Galatians, chapter 2, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Galatia, explaining to them that he was accepted by the apostles and that he opposes Cephas and all the apostles who knew that we are not justified by the Law, but by faith in Christ. Yet, even so, they continue to demand in the presence of other Jews (to win their favor) that non-Jews must follow the laws of Judaism—which is hypocrisy.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **The apostles accept Paul.** Paul explains at the beginning of the letter that when he went to meet the apostles for the first time with Barnabas, he was well received and accepted by them. They accepted him as a brother and an apostle of Christ. Paul explained to them how he came to receive the gospel of Christ by revelation from Christ Himself and that he was tasked with preaching the gospel to non-Jews (Galatians 2:1–2, 6–9). Despite Paul's former persecution of Christians, they were convinced by Paul's testimony and what he preached. Paul was preaching the exact same gospel as they were. Furthermore, he had received the spiritual gifts of the word of wisdom, the word of knowledge, and faith. Thanks to this, he could explain and clarify mysteries and doubts that many had, which not all apostles could do as effectively. This was also due to the fact that Paul was called by Christ to be His apostle to non-Jews (even though he was a Jew and a Pharisee). This gave him a great advantage and perspectives that many lacked. That is, Paul was raised as a Pharisee and mastered the Law just like the teachers of the Law; but he also persecuted Christians and became one of the greatest apostles of Christ. These experiences offered Paul a perspective, wisdom, and knowledge that almost no one else possessed. For this reason, he is considered one of the most important apostles (if not the most important of all).
2. **Paul opposes the apostles who preached to non-Jews that they must obey the Laws of Judaism to win the favor of Jews, when they themselves know that we are justified by faith.** Paul was able to perceive that Cephas (one of the apostles) was acting hypocritically regarding his conduct, and he rebuked him. Paul did not want non-Jews to be harmed or to stumble due to the unworthy conduct of some Christians. Moreover, he did not seek to win the favor of men (but rather that of God). Cephas conveyed conflicting messages when he ate with non-Jews in the absence of other Jews, but later stopped eating with them, withdrew, and preached the Law in the presence of Jews (Galatians 2:11–13). This is conduct unworthy of a Christian, and Paul was right to confront him. Cephas, who was a Jew, was no longer living as one, yet he was compelling Gentiles to practice Judaism (Galatians 2:14). We must be very careful with this as Christians. Our conduct must be consistent with our faith in Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior so that we do not continue sinning against Christ (John 14:21), and so that we do not cause others to stumble through our fault (Luke 17:1–2).
