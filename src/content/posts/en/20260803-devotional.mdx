# Devotional Acts 24: Paul Before Felix and Divine Justice

Today's devotional is found in the book of Acts, chapter 24, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the high priest Ananias, together with the religious leaders, went down to Caesarea for Paul's trial, which was presided over by the governor, Felix. Ananias begins by greeting the governor and thanking him for the years of peace Felix brought to the region. He then accuses Paul of inciting riots among the Jews and profaning the temple by bringing Gentiles into a sacred space. However, when it was Paul's turn to defend himself, Paul answered that, firstly, none of those present accusing him had any credibility, since they were not eyewitnesses to what he had done—it was actually the Jews from Asia who had raised the complaint (meaning the present accusers knew nothing firsthand, only what they had heard). Secondly, since they did not witness anything and could not substantiate any of their accusations, they would have to admit that they were pursuing him simply because he believed in the resurrection of the dead. Paul openly admitted that he believed in the resurrection of the dead and followed The Way, which is Jesus Christ and which the Jews referred to as a sect. Upon hearing this, the governor decided to adjourn the proceedings until the commander, Lysias, arrived to resolve the case. However, while waiting for the commander, Felix and his wife summoned Paul on several occasions to hear him speak about faith in Christ, righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come. Yet, two years after the initial hearing, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus, and wanting to grant a favor to the Jews, Felix left Paul in prison.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **The hypocrisy of the Sadducees.** The Sadducees were a priestly class responsible for carrying out the rituals, sacrifices, and services in the temple. In other words, they knew the priestly and Mosaic laws very well. They knew they were commanded by God not to give false testimony against anyone (Exodus 20:16 NIV) and not to strike anyone in the temple so as not to defile it (Ezekiel 22:26 NIV). Yet, this was precisely what Ananias did when he ordered someone near Paul to strike him on the mouth during his trial before the Sanhedrin (Acts 23:2–3 NIV). And it was also what Ananias did by giving false testimony against Paul during the hearing in Caesarea, despite never having witnessed Paul causing any disturbance (Acts 24:12–13 NIV). Unfortunately, this is something that still happens today among church leaders and is one of the causes of alienation between believers and God (Romans 2:17–24 NIV).
2. **Human justice is flawed.** We notice in this chapter that human justice will never be perfect or fair in the true sense of the word. For despite having no evidence to accuse Paul, they brought charges against him anyway, and even then, he was not exonerated but remained imprisoned (Acts 24:13, 23 NIV). Furthermore, as if that were not enough, two years later, Felix kept him imprisoned simply to stay in the good graces of the Jews, not because any evidence had been found regarding the charges leveled against Paul (Acts 24:27 NIV). This is an excellent example of why God's justice is perfect—it is the only true and righteous justice, capable of judging each person according to their own actions (Deuteronomy 32:4 NIV). Therefore, whenever we cry out for justice in this world, we must remember that it is precisely because true justice does not exist on earth that we hunger and thirst for divine justice (Matthew 5:6 NIV).
