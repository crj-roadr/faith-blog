# Devotional Philemon: Does God Approve of Slavery? A Biblical Analysis

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philemon, chapter 1, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter to his son in the faith, Philemon, thanking God for his love and faithfulness to Christ. Furthermore, Paul uses this same love that Philemon has for Christ to plead with him to welcome Onesimus back—no longer as his slave, but as a free man and a brother in Christ Jesus.

## Observations

1. **God does not approve of slavery, as some claim.** This chapter, in which Paul pleads with Philemon to accept Onesimus back not as a slave but as a free man and his brother in Christ, is one of the proofs that God does not endorse slavery. But this is not the only evidence we find in the Bible. Many people use Old Testament passages to argue that God approved of slavery and that He changed in the New Testament. Passages such as those found in Leviticus 25:44–46, where God permits the purchase of slaves from neighboring nations or from foreigners living among the Israelites, treating them as hereditary property for their families. Or passages like Exodus 21:20–21, where God permits masters to beat their slaves as long as they do not kill them. These are frequently used to justify this belief. But what those people always forget is the context. God did not pre-approve slavery when He created the universe. When God created the universe, it was perfect, because sin and death did not yet exist (Genesis 1:31). It was because of Adam and Eve—and subsequently ourselves—that sin entered the world, bringing with it death and all kinds of depravity (Genesis 3:18–22; 6:5–6). God simply took the sinful practices that human beings already displayed due to their inclination toward evil and began to contain them (setting limits), with the ultimate aim of transforming our hearts of stone into hearts of flesh (Ezekiel 36:26–27)—that is, giving us new hearts so that we would stop wanting to satisfy the desires of the flesh and start wanting to satisfy the desires of the Spirit (Galatians 5:16–17). However, others also claim that Paul himself in the New Testament promotes slavery by instructing slaves to obey their earthly masters and by telling masters to treat them with love and justice, acknowledging a framework of spiritual equality before God (Ephesians 6:5–9; Colossians 3:22). But once again, what they forget is the context. Both God and Paul were working to abolish slavery, but they could not simply force people to abandon it, because doing so would override their free will. That is why the strategy they employ is transformation—so that people, as Christians, would voluntarily leave slavery of their own free will. We can see examples of this approach in both the Old and New Testaments. Passages where God commands that slaves be freed in the seventh year (the Jubilee year) after six years of service, and that they be released generously (Exodus 21:2–6; Deuteronomy 15:12–15). And passages like those found in this chapter, where Paul pleads with Philemon to receive Onesimus as a free brother in Christ (Philemon 1:8–21). Therefore, the belief that God approves of slavery is false—He does not approve of it in the Old Testament or in the New.
