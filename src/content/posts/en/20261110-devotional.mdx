# Devotional Psalm 119:17-24: Open My Eyes to the Wonders of Your Law

Today's devotional is found in the book of Psalms, chapter 119, verses 17–24, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In these verses of this chapter, the psalmist pleads with God to deal bountifully with him, to open his eyes so that he may behold the wonders of His Law, and to remove reproach and contempt from him because he keeps His testimonies.

## Observations

1. **We must plead with God to open our eyes so that we may learn to appreciate the wonders of His Law.** This plea of the psalmist is one that we all need to make. For by nature, we have an inclination to disobey God's commandments and His laws. This is because we corrupted our original good essence in the sight of God; that is, we ceased to have hearts inclined to seek purity and holiness. But in the beginning, we were not like this; we possessed a purer nature. All of this began when Adam and Eve sinned against God. God commanded Adam that they were forbidden to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for in the day they ate of it, they would surely die (Genesis 2:15–17). Nevertheless, Adam and Eve disobeyed God. Consequently, sin and death entered the world (Genesis 3:17, 21–22). This contributed to the corruption of our pure nature. That is why we now possess a sinful nature that desires to satisfy the cravings of the flesh rather than the desires of the Spirit (Galatians 5:16–17). This is why the psalmist cries out to God to open his eyes and make him perceive that following His ways is best for us. For His ways lead us to salvation, whereas our own ways lead us to destruction. For this reason, we must pray and implore God to replace our hearts of stone with hearts of flesh and put His Spirit within us (Ezekiel 36:26). Only with His Spirit can we begin to desire what pleases the Spirit and truly appreciate God's commandments. And in order to receive the Holy Spirit, we must first repent of our sins and, second, accept Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior (Ephesians 1:13). From that point onward, we begin the process of transformation from our sinful nature toward a holy nature, and consequently, we learn to appreciate the wonders of God's Law.
