# Devotional Colossians 1: The Supremacy of Christ and Reconciliation

Today's devotional is found in the book of Colossians, chapter 1, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Colossae, explaining to them the supremacy of Jesus Christ as co-creator of all things and reconciler of all creation. Furthermore, Paul also tells them about the thanksgivings and intercessory prayers he has offered for them because of the love and faith they have for Christ. Finally, Paul explains to them what the goal of his work for the church is—to proclaim and teach the gospel of Christ to all nations.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **The supremacy of Christ as co-creator of all and reconciler of humanity.** When Paul makes this statement and we read it for the first time, we understand why he refers to Jesus Christ as reconciler. However, what can sometimes cause confusion is the statement that through Jesus all things were created (Colossians 1:15–16). Depending on our knowledge and wisdom in the Holy Scriptures, it is easy to see why some do not understand that Jesus was co-creator, with God the Father, of all things. Yet, the evidence that corroborates this statement of Paul is entirely in the Bible (in both the Old and New Testaments). The first passages where Jesus Christ is mentioned, implicitly, as co-creator of all creation with God are found in the book of John, chapter 1, verses 1–3. In these verses, John said that in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Furthermore, he also mentions that through Him (i.e., the Word) all things were created, and without Him nothing that was created would exist. In these passages, which belong to the gospel of Christ written by John, we can discern that the Word refers to Jesus Christ and the Word is the speech (i.e., the spoken word). We also perceive that Jesus (i.e., the Word) is God. I imagine that, right now, you might be asking: How can Jesus be both a man and God? The answer is that the God of the Bible, although one, exists in 3 distinct persons (i.e., God the Father, God the Son, and the Holy Spirit). When John speaks of the Word, he refers to God the Son who manifests, in the spiritual dimension, as God the Son and the Word of God, and, in the material dimension (on earth), as Jesus Christ and the Son of Man. Moreover, Jesus here on earth was both man and God. First, because He possessed all authority in heaven and on earth and commanded His disciples to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:18–19). Second, because He Himself forgave the sins of those who repented before Him—an exclusive prerogative of God (Matthew 9:2–6). Third, because He accepted worship from people—another exclusive attribute of God (Matthew 14:33, 15:25, and 28:9; John 9:38, 20:28; and Luke 24:52). Fourth, because the reason for which Jesus was condemned to death by crucifixion was for blasphemy by declaring that He is the Son of God (Matthew 26:59–66). These are some of the proofs that Jesus was both man and God. Furthermore, evidence that God is one and exists in 3 distinct persons, and that Jesus is God, is also found in the Old Testament (Genesis 1:26–27, 15:1; Numbers 24:2–3; Jeremiah 1:11; Ezekiel 33:1; Jonah 1:1, 3:3; Psalm 33:6; Isaiah 55:11; 2 Samuel 7:12–14; Proverbs 30:4). Finally, Jesus is the reconciler of all humanity, which had been prophesied since the Old Testament and was fulfilled in the New Testament (Genesis 12:1–3, 22:18; Isaiah 7:14, 53:5–6; Micah 5:2; and Daniel 7:13–14). For this reason, Jesus said that God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life (John 3:16).
