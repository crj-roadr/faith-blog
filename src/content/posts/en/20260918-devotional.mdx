# Devotional 2 Corinthians 10: Defense of Ministry and Spiritual Weapons

Today's devotional is found in the book of 2 Corinthians, chapter 10, and this was what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his second letter to the church of Corinth, mounting a defense of his apostolic ministry against critics who opposed his authority and questioned his manner of proclaiming the gospel.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **The vital importance of deeply knowing God's Word to minister and defend the gospel of Christ.** In this chapter, Paul addresses those who sought to undermine his ministry. Critics claimed that he was timid and unimpressive when present in person, but harsh and demanding in his letters, even disparaging his speaking ability (2 Corinthians 10:1, 9–10). Yet Paul refused to fight back using worldly tactics or carnal weapons (2 Corinthians 10:3–5). Instead, he relied on the divine power of the Holy Spirit, responding to personal attacks with the meekness and gentleness of Christ. Every believer who seeks to follow Jesus faithfully will encounter misunderstanding, criticism, and opposition. Our calling is to immerse ourselves in the Scriptures, follow Christ's footsteps, and proclaim His gospel with integrity. As long as our lives align with Christ's teachings and we avoid becoming a stumbling block to others, such hostility remains baseless (John 15:10 and Matthew 18:6–7). To the worldly mindset, spiritual truths appear as foolishness, because they are spiritually discerned (1 Corinthians 2:14). Furthermore, Jesus prepared us for this reality, reminding us that a servant is not greater than his master; if the world persecuted our Lord, it will persecute His followers as well (John 15:20). In fact, enduring reproach for the name of Christ confirms that the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon us (1 Peter 4:14). Therefore, we must equip our minds and hearts with the wisdom of the Word, so that we may withstand accusations, defend our faith with grace, and honor Christ without alienating seekers from God.
