# Devotional Galatians 4: Children of the Free Woman and Freedom in Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of Galatians, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Galatia, expressing his deep concern regarding their spiritual relapse after receiving the gospel, despite having initially been so eager and enthusiastic to know it. Furthermore, Paul explains to them—using an analogy between Hagar and Sarah—how we were previously children of the slave woman, but now that we have accepted the gospel, we are children of the free woman (children of God and heirs of the promise).

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **Paul's concern with the conduct of the Galatians after receiving the gospel.** Paul could not understand how the attitude of the Galatians changed so dramatically overnight. For before receiving the gospel of Jesus, they were very enthusiastic, hungering and thirsting to know it. But after receiving it, they stopped prioritizing it and began considering going back to living under the Law (Galatians 4:13–16). However, Paul perceived that this was not happening through their own independent decisions. They were being influenced by other people who did not have the best intentions. Because of this, many Galatians stopped listening to the gospel message Paul wrote to them and began viewing him as an enemy. What happened to them is very similar to what happens to the seeds that fall along the path and on rocky ground in Jesus' parable of the sower (Matthew 13:3–9, 18–21). For some of them likely received the gospel, but did not understand it; so ill-intentioned people came along and deceived them. Meanwhile, others received it and understood it with joy, but because they lacked sufficiently deep spiritual roots, tribulations and persecutions drove them away from the gospel. This is a very important observation because the same can happen to us. For this reason, we must seek to draw near to God every single day through prayer and Bible reading. We need a solid biblical foundation and great faith in order to receive the gospel properly and never stray from it.
2. **The children of the slave woman (Hagar) and the children of the free woman (Sarah).** In this letter, Paul also makes this observation, which had never occurred to me until reading it. He explains the difference between the children of both women, how they were conceived, and what their destinies are (Galatians 4:21–23). In Hagar's case, she was Sarah's slave. Therefore, her children were automatically born already condemned to slavery just like their mother, conceived through human methods and traditions (Galatians 4:24–25). That is, she came to conceive Ishmael because, up to that point, Sarah was barren and unable to bear children for Abraham. Therefore, following customary practice, Sarah recommended and allowed Abraham to sleep with Hagar to finally obtain offspring (Genesis 16:1–3). In other words, all of this was done through reasons and methods established by human beings. In Sarah's case, however, she was Abraham's wife. Although barren and unable to bear offspring for Abraham, if she were to have children with Abraham, they would be free. And that is precisely what happened when God appeared to Abraham and told him that in a year's time, he would have a son (offspring) through Sarah by God's blessing. A year later, Sarah became pregnant with Isaac, the son God had promised to Abraham (Genesis 17:15–19). Thus, all of this was a miracle and came about through God's divine blessing. This difference between the two women is what Paul highlights and compares to our lives here on earth before and after Christ. For before Jesus, we lived under the Law, and since we could not fulfill it, we were imprisoned and condemned to remain slaves to sin through the Law, just like Hagar's children. However, after Christ, we no longer need to fulfill the entire law to obtain salvation, because Christ did it for us. Therefore, we are now free thanks to Jesus' sacrifice, and we begin living by faith and the Spirit (Galatians 4:28–31). This is why we can never be justified by the Law: we could never keep it, but Jesus fulfilled it so that we could receive salvation whenever we place our faith in Him as our Lord and Savior.
