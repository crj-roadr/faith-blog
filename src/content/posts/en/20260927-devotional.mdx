# Devotional Galatians 6: Mutual Help and Warning Against False Teachers

Today's devotional is found in the book of Galatians, chapter 6, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Galatia, explaining how they ought to help one another carry their burdens. Furthermore, those who are spiritual should help those who are less spiritual restore themselves from their sins with humility. Finally, Paul warns the Galatians to beware of those Christians who compel them to be circumcised, for this is no longer mandatory and they only force it to please worldly authorities in order to avoid being persecuted for the cross of Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **Mutual help among Christians as support in spiritual restoration from sin.** This is a very important observation. We often love to judge others harshly for the sins they commit, as though we ourselves were not sinners just like them—or even worse. The severity with which we treat others when catching them in sin is reprehensible and is precisely what Paul condemns. That is not the right attitude when dealing with someone caught in sin. Instead, we must seek to restore that person in a spirit of humility, humbling ourselves before them (Galatians 6:1). Paul encourages us to carry each other's burdens (sins) to the cross in order to fulfill the law of Christ (Galatians 6:2). After all, we are all sinners, and no one should compare their own sins to the sins of others. Such comparisons lead people to relativize sin, measure which sin is worse than another, and pridefully consider themselves better than their neighbors. This is the spiritual blindness that stems from legalistic religiosity (Matthew 7:3–5). Furthermore, when we judge others for their sins, we fail to follow Jesus' example. Jesus did not condemn the woman caught in adultery, but rather told her to go and sin no more (John 8:10–11). Above all, with the measure we use to judge others, it will be measured to us by God as well (Matthew 7:2).
2. **Beware of Christians who preach a modified gospel to please authorities and avoid persecution for the cross of Christ.** This warning is of utmost importance; we must know how to identify these types of people. They act as stumbling blocks in our walk of holiness with the Holy Spirit (John 17:1–2). These are the false prophets Jesus warned the apostles about, instructing them to be careful not to be deceived by their flattering words and appearances (Matthew 7:15–20). They manage to deceive the masses by exploiting our ignorance and lack of biblical knowledge and wisdom, twisting the message to win the favor of elites and ruling authorities. For this reason, the only way to protect ourselves against such deception is to know the Word of God thoroughly. We must read the Bible, meditate on its words, and ask God for wisdom and understanding to guard ourselves against these deceivers. After gaining deeper knowledge of God's ways, we should ask the Holy Spirit for the gift of discerning spirits so that we can distinguish good spirits from bad ones. For a tree is recognized by its fruit: a good tree never bears bad fruit, nor does a bad tree bear good fruit. In the same way, with growing spiritual maturity, we will be able to distinguish those who walk by the Spirit from those who walk by the flesh.
