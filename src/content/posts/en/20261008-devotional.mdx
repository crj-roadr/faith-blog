# Devotional Philippians 4: The Power of Gratitude and Good Cheer

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philippians, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul concludes his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, thanking them from the bottom of his heart for all the help he received from them. Explaining to them that he is not thanking them to receive more things, but to increase the credit to their account.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Give thanks for all the help we receive both from God and from our neighbor.** This is a very powerful observation and changes how we live our lives. But it is not always easy to put into practice. For life is made of ups and downs. And the worst part is that the downs linger in our minds longer than the ups. Furthermore, sometimes it seems that bad things happen all at once like a tsunami of problems. Which gives us the feeling that we are drowning in so many problems we have to solve. Because of these problems, we complain, grow faint, and sometimes point the finger at God—which is totally wrong. While all of this happens, we forget the blessings we have every day. Starting, precisely, with the blessing of life. For every day we wake up, there are other people who no longer enjoy this privilege. For having the breath of life is a privilege and a gift that no one deserved. God wanted to give us the opportunity to be born and to be able to share love with God and our neighbor. Therefore, whenever we wake up and are still alive, we must give thanks to God, even when our current situation is precarious—because things can always get worse. Furthermore, we must praise God for this, for it is He who keeps us alive (Job 12:10). For no one can add even an hour to their life no matter how much they try if God did not want it to be so (Matthew 6:27). We must also thank all the people who help us every day (e.g., our parents, siblings, relatives, friends, acquaintances, strangers, etc.). For these people are not obligated to help us, because God created us with free will and it is up to each one to decide how to use it (Genesis 1:26–27). Therefore, the fact that every day we have electricity at home, internet, food to eat, cars to drive, smartphones to communicate in real time, doctors and nurses to heal us, judges, police officers, and lawyers to protect us, etc., does not mean we always will. For no one knows the future. For these and many other reasons, we must practice gratitude every day, with good cheer, for the big things as well as the small. For Jesus warned us that we would have tribulations in our lives, and even though we go through them, we must take heart because He overcame the world to save us (John 16:33).
