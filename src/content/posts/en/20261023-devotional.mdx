# Devotional 1 Timothy 5: Family Responsibility and Active Faith

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Timothy, chapter 5, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his first letter to his son in faith, Timothy, instructing him on how the church should treat widows, elders, and religious leaders. Paul directs that the responsibility of caring for one's own should rest with church members themselves. That is, to treat an older man as a father, an older woman as a mother, a younger man as a brother, and a younger woman as a sister. If any widow in the church has children or grandchildren, they should be the ones to care for her, honouring their faith and relieving the burden from the church. Thus the church takes on responsibility for the truly destitute widows who have placed their hope in God and persevere in prayer and supplication day and night.

## Observations

1. **Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.** This statement by Paul is the purest truth — even if it is hard for some to admit. As Christians, Jesus Christ explained to us what the two greatest commandments are. The first and greatest of all is to love God with all your being — that is, to love Him with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength. The second is to love your neighbour as yourself. According to Christ, these are so important that all the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments (Matthew 22:36–40). Furthermore, whoever claims to love God but hates their neighbour is a liar. For one cannot say they love God, whom they have not seen, and hate their neighbour, whom they have seen (1 John 4:20–21). In other words, we cannot, as Christians, say we love God and at the same time refuse to help those around us who are in need — our parents, siblings, grandparents, spouses, children, relatives, friends, and so on. For whoever truly loves God is concerned with helping the people God loves. I know some may counter this and justify why they do not help a certain person — because that person betrayed them, mistreated them, stole from them, abused them, and so forth. It is genuinely very difficult to forgive someone who has caused so much pain in our lives. I understand that perspective. However, that perspective is ours — a human one. For us, it makes sense to help those who help us and let those who harm us suffer. But this is precisely what Jesus condemns. For according to Jesus, we must always forgive those who wrong us, just as Jesus forgave us when we betrayed Him without cause (Matthew 18:21–22). Yet Jesus goes far beyond simply forgiving those who harm us. He commands us to love everyone — including our enemies — as we would want them to treat us (Luke 6:27–31). Because according to Jesus, there is no merit in loving only those who love us, for even sinners do that. Therefore we must help our enemies without expecting anything in return. For it is only when we are compassionate as God is compassionate that we are truly His children, because He is kind even to the ungrateful and wicked (Luke 6:32–36).
