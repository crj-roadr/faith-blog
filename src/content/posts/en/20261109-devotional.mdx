# Devotional Hebrews 8: Jesus and the Mediator of a New Covenant

Today's devotional is found in the book of Hebrews, chapter 8, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, the author writes a letter addressed to Christians of Jewish background, explaining to them why Jesus is the High Priest and mediator of a far superior new covenant.

## Observations

1. **Jesus is the High Priest who sat down at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, serving in the true tabernacle set up by the Lord and not by mere human hands.** As a rule, earthly priests were appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices in an earthly sanctuary, which was merely a shadow and copy of the heavenly reality. Therefore, Jesus was not a priest according to the old Levitical law, but a High Priest in the order of Melchizedek, whose priesthood is eternal and whose offering was Himself to atone once for all for the sins of humanity—past, present, and future (Hebrews 8:5–6). The very fact that Christ came to establish a new covenant between God and humanity demonstrates that the first covenant was not faultless, which is why a second covenant was enacted in its place (Hebrews 8:7). The author details Jesus' role in this new covenant so that his original audience (Jewish Christians) would recognize that Jesus is indeed God's promised Christ. He explains that Jesus is the true Messiah whom the Jewish people had eagerly anticipated for generations. For in Judaism, both then and now, Jesus Christ is not recognized as God or the Messiah, but merely as a historic Jewish teacher or rabbi. Thus, the author uses the Old Testament scriptures and ceremonial law to show how Jesus fulfills every messianic prophecy and requirement. This equipped Jewish believers with biblical arguments, sound evidence, and theological depth to defend their faith in Christ as Lord and Savior. Inevitably, many around them would seek to dissuade them from Christianity and draw them back into traditional Judaism, while others would genuinely inquire about the evidence pointing to Jesus as the Messiah (1 Peter 3:15). This remains our duty as Christians today: to know the facts, reasons, and scriptural evidence that corroborate our faith and enable us to give a reasoned defense of the hope within us.
