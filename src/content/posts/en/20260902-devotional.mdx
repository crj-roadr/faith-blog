# Devotional 1 Corinthians 10: Flee from Idolatry and Freedom

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Corinthians, chapter 10, and this was what happened.

**Synopsis:** Paul warns in his letter to the church of Corinth about the following themes: the example from Israel's history that we should not follow, that we must flee from idolatry, and the care we must take with Christian freedom.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **The example from Israel's history that we should not follow.** Paul explains to the Christians in Corinth that we should all look at what happened to the Israelites as an example of what not to follow (1 Corinthians 10:11). For they disobeyed God's commandments, statutes, and precepts countless times. Starting with grumbling after crossing the Red Sea when they did not yet have water to drink (Exodus 17:2–3). They also complained about the lack of food in the Wilderness of Sin and said they would have preferred to die in Egypt where they could eat their fill (Exodus 16:2–3). They even reached the point of asking Aaron to make a golden calf so they could worship it, since Moses went up to speak with God and had not yet returned (Exodus 32:1–6). Not to mention that they failed to learn the lesson and continued committing many other sins even after being saved from death through Moses' intercession with God. That is why Paul said that God was not pleased with most of them, and consequently their bodies were scattered in the wilderness (1 Corinthians 10:5). However, the warning Paul gave to the church in Corinth applies to us as well. We must remember that all these people were disobedient and therefore perished. If we desire eternal life, we must walk in the ways of God and follow in the footsteps of Jesus.
2. **We must flee from idolatry.** This observation is very important, because there is confusion about what truly is the opposite of a theist. People usually say that the opposite of a theist is an atheist. But this is not necessarily true. Although it is the word commonly associated as the antonym of theist, it is not the most accurate one. For it does not take human nature into consideration. Every human being was created with a void that can only be filled by God (Blaise Pascal, *Pensées* VII (425)). That is why we endlessly seek something in material things to fill us with happiness and purpose. When the only entity capable of filling this void is God, because He is infinite, almighty, omniscient, and eternal. That being said, we can now better perceive what the true antonym of a theist or believer is—an idolater. Idolatry is the true opposite of being a theist or believer. For an idolater is a person who places whatever is not God as the most important thing in their life. That is why we must be very careful with idolatry. For it not only distances us from God, but is also in direct opposition to love, worship, and praise for God.
3. **The care we must take with Christian freedom.** Paul also warns them that we must be careful with our Christian freedom. For being entrusted with great power also brings greater responsibility. And the great responsibility we must fulfill is to avoid being a stumbling block to others. For although there are many things we can do because we have the wisdom and spiritual maturity to know they are not sin, we should avoid doing them in the presence of others. So as not to lead those who lack sufficient knowledge into sin by reaching wrong conclusions about what they think they can do based on what they saw us doing (1 Corinthians 8:9–13). In other words, we must use our freedom not for our own interests, but for the interests of our neighbor so that they may be saved. Therefore, we should seek to glorify God in everything we do (1 Corinthians 10:31–33).
