# Devotional 1 Thessalonians 4: The Coming of the Lord and the Rapture

Today's devotional is found in the book of 1 Thessalonians, chapter 4, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter, addressed to all believers in Christ Jesus who are in Thessalonica, about how all believers will be caught up to be with Jesus Christ forever. He also explains that they should not be discouraged like those who have already lost hope in the resurrection of the dead.

## Observations

1. **The coming of the Lord.** The return of Jesus Christ will be the most important date in the history of humanity. For it will be the moment when no one will be able to deny His existence any longer, because it will be as evident as the things we observe every day (Matthew 24:30). That said, as Christians we must reflect very carefully on all the implications of Jesus' return. First, because Jesus is coming precisely to put a definitive end to the evil that has been established on earth. That is, all those who practiced and practice the works of the flesh, doing Satan's will, will be destroyed and cast into hell with him (Matthew 25:41). These words of Jesus should serve as a warning for us who do not want this end. And this applies to both believers and non-believers. As believers, we must always be watching and ready for the return of Jesus, for no one knows when the day of His coming will be (Matthew 24:36, 42–44). In other words, we must get off the fence and decide, once and for all, whether we are going to live our lives to satisfy the desires of the Spirit or the desires of the flesh. Because the two oppose each other and cannot coexist (Galatians 5:16–17). Second, for the salvation of our loved ones and all human beings. That is, if we truly believe in Jesus Christ, we must keep His commandments and spread His gospel (John 14:21 and Mark 16:15). For the eternal life of all our friends, relatives, and non-believers is at stake. And we have received the best news and cure for the greatest evil that pursues everyone—death. Therefore, we must have compassion and love for our neighbor, helping them reconcile with Jesus and accept Him as Lord and Savior, so that they do not spend eternity in hell. This is the hope that we all have as Christians, for we know that we will not see our loved ones only in this life but also in the one to come. And there is no news capable of comforting us more than this. For as Paul said in this chapter: when Jesus returns, first the dead will rise, and only afterward will both those who were resurrected and those who are still alive be caught up in the clouds to meet Jesus (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17).
