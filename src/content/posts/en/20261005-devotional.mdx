# Devotional Philippians 2: The Humility of Christ and His Exaltation

Today's devotional is found in the book of Philippians, chapter 2, verses 1–11, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes in his letter addressed to all the believers in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, exhorting them to be humble so that they may be exalted, following the supreme example of Jesus Christ.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **Humility leads to exaltation.** This is an observation that is often not well received in today's world. In the era of social media, what most consider good and impressive is having massive exposure and followers to validate their worth in society, or having great wealth and flaunting fortune to gain the approval of others. This is nothing new: throughout the Bible, many ancient civilizations prioritized human customs and human approval while rejecting the guidance and approval of God. This is a phenomenon of human nature resulting from our fallen condition. Every human being has a natural tendency to become enslaved to something or someone. That is why Jesus said that no one can serve two masters at the same time, for they will devote themselves to one and despise the other (Matthew 6:24). In other words, one cannot serve both God and money or human praise. The more we live to serve one, the more we love it and disregard the other. It was no coincidence that Paul explained that we cannot live to satisfy both the desires of the flesh and the desires of the Spirit. We must choose, because the two cannot coexist. While we live according to one, the other weakens and dies within us (Galatians 5:16–17). What we must do to be blessed and exalted in Christ is to follow His example and walk in humility. But when humility is spoken of, many wonder: how can one be exalted by being humble? Are not the humble the ones who get trampled on most? The answer is both yes and no. The answer is yes when we do things seeking human approval. If we are humble merely to receive the praise and validation of men, there will indeed be many who mock and take advantage of our humility. But the answer is no when we do the will of God for His glory. For when we do things for the glory of God, we are laying up treasures in the world to come, not necessarily in this one—though God may also grant blessings in this life. Moreover, storing up treasures in heaven is the wisest decision anyone can ever make. Heavenly treasures do not rot, and no thief can steal them—unlike the fleeting riches accumulated here on earth (Matthew 6:19–20). Furthermore, where our treasure is, there our heart will be also, revealing whether we seek to please God or men (Matthew 6:21). As Christians, we are called to follow the example of Christ, who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to His own advantage. Instead, He humbled Himself, taking the form of a servant, and came into the world as a human being to serve and sacrifice Himself for us for the glory of God. Precisely because He did this, God exalted Him to the highest place and gave Him the name that is above every name, both in heaven and on earth (Philippians 2:5–11). And according to Christ, God will do the same with us. That is why Jesus declared that the first will be last, and the last will be first (Matthew 19:30 and Mark 9:35), and that blessed are the meek and humble, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven (Matthew 5:3, 5). Even in everyday life on earth, truly humble people end up being more respected and honored. We all have had experiences with people who constantly brag about their achievements, and those around them instantly feel put off by such arrogance. In contrast, when someone is genuinely humble and does not boast about their achievements, others who know them speak on their behalf—earning genuine respect and admiration from everyone listening. That is why Proverbs advises us to let another praise you, and not your own mouth (Proverbs 27:2). For when we boast of ourselves, we lose the respect of others. Therefore, humility is the true path to exaltation, both in heaven and on earth.
