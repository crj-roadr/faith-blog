# Devotional James 5: Patience in Suffering and the Power of Prayer

Today's devotional is found in the book of James, chapter 5, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, James writes a letter addressed to the Christians of the twelve tribes of Israel scattered throughout the world, warning oppressive rich people about the consequences of their greed, exhorting distressed Christians to have patience like the prophets in the face of suffering, and counseling them to pray about their problems and for one another.

## Observations

1. **We must be generous with our wealth and abandon greed.** Unfortunately, in an era of unprecedented wealth, we still grapple with a major greed problem. There are many individuals who possess goods and money that they will never be able to spend in their lifetime or the lifetimes of their descendants. They prefer hoarding wealth in their hands to feed personal vices when they could be more generous and support those in need. For as Jesus told His disciples, whenever someone refuses to help the needy, they are refusing to help Jesus (Matthew 25:45). This is a sin against God, violating the second greatest commandment according to Jesus Christ, which is to love our neighbor as ourselves (Matthew 22:39). Many forget that no one can take their riches when they die, and that wealth left behind can be stolen, rot, or decay (Matthew 6:19–21). Furthermore, this earthly life is not our final destination; an eternity awaits us all. We must never lose sight of this reality. Therefore, we ought to be generous and help others just as we would wish others to help us in our times of need.
2. **We must cultivate patience in the face of suffering, just like the prophets.** This is perhaps the greatest challenge for any Christian. And it is one of the reasons why walking the Christian path is far from easy, contrary to what some assume. Being a Christian means placing our hopes in God in everything we do, especially during tribulations. That is, we must stop thinking we can do everything on our own and learn to rely fully on divine provision. This implies showing up and loving others even when under tremendous pressure and stress. It implies maintaining good cheer even when circumstances look bleak. It implies being patient even when we cannot yet see the light at the end of the tunnel. These are demanding attitudes to sustain when the world around us appears to be crumbling. Even so, we must remain faithful and obedient to God, just as the prophets did. For God may seem slow to our timing, but He never fails (2 Peter 3:9; Numbers 23:19).
3. **We must pray for our own needs and the needs of our neighbors.** This is an extraordinary privilege and blessing, even if not everyone sees it that way. It is as if we possessed the personal contact information of the wisest, most powerful beings who could assist us at any moment, and we could reach out to them whenever necessary. But in our case, it is infinitely better: God is the Creator of all things, almighty, omnipresent, omniscient, and capable of attending to all of us simultaneously (Jeremiah 32:17; Matthew 19:26; Proverbs 15:3; Psalm 139:1–4). Furthermore, God loves us so deeply that He grants us the privilege of a direct relationship with Him through prayer, even knowing that we are sinners and will continue to stumble. Even under these conditions, God is exceedingly merciful, gracious, and slow to anger (Psalm 145:8–9). Therefore, we must continually place our struggles into God's hands, asking for His guidance, help, and discernment, knowing He has promised to hear our prayers (Psalm 34:17; Proverbs 15:29).
