# Devotional James 3: The Power of the Tongue and Divine Wisdom

Today's devotional is found in the book of James, chapter 3, and this is what happened.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, James writes a letter addressed to the Christians of the twelve tribes of Israel scattered throughout the world, explaining to them why we must tame our own tongue and the difference between the two kinds of wisdom.

## Observations

1. **We must be careful with the tongue so as not to bless God and with the same tongue curse human beings created by God.** This is a struggle that nearly all of us face in one way or another. No matter how much we attempt to control our tongue on our own strength, we cannot do so permanently. Some have the discipline to hold their tongue longer than others, but we all end up using our tongue to curse the very human beings whom Jesus commanded us to love as ourselves. We see this tendency to slander others when discussing politics and disagreeing with the policies of a government, party, or politician. We also see it when someone betrays us deeply and we become enraged with that person. We also witness this when visiting an establishment or requesting a product or service and being treated rudely. In all these situations, almost everyone has said or harbored bitter thoughts about the person in question. But this is precisely what we must cease doing, fighting daily to rein in and ultimately tame our tongue. We can fool others, but we can never fool God. For Jesus and Scripture remind us that one cannot claim to love God while hating or cursing one's brother (1 John 4:20–21). The true way we demonstrate our love for God is by obeying His commandments (John 14:15).
2. **We must know how to distinguish between the two kinds of wisdom (i.e., heavenly wisdom and earthly wisdom).** This is profound truth, yet we only perceive and grasp it when we truly come to know the ways and will of God. If someone lacks this biblical understanding, they will fail to notice the contrast. For earthly wisdom makes logical sense to our minds, which are inherently prone to sin, whereas the directives of God and Jesus sound bizarre to someone who does not seek a close relationship with Them (1 Corinthians 2:14, 3:18–20). For example, according to worldly wisdom, we should love those who love us and never love those who hate us. But according to Jesus Christ, we are to love everyone, including our enemies (Matthew 5:44 and Luke 6:27–28). According to worldly wisdom, we should give a person at most three chances; if they betray us a fourth time, we should never forgive them. But according to Jesus Christ, we are to forgive seventy times seven times those who wrong us—meaning continually (Matthew 18:21–22). According to worldly wisdom, when a couple divorces under civil law, their marriage is completely dissolved because no legal bond remains between the spouses. But according to Jesus Christ, a marriage is not dissolved simply because civil authorities grant a divorce, for in God's eyes there is a spiritual bond forged when God joins two people into one flesh. Only in cases of sexual immorality (i.e., marital unfaithfulness) is this covenant broken (Matthew 5:31–32). As we can see, these are just a few of many examples demonstrating how radically different both wisdoms are. Therefore, as Christians, the wisdom we must pursue and heed is God's wisdom, not popular culture's. For whenever we follow the teachings of the world, we will most often find ourselves sinning against God.
