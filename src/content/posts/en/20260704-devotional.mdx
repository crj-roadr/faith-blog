# Devotional John 18: The Arrest of Jesus and the Trial Before Pilate

I finished today's devotional and it was on chapter 18 of the book of John.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Jesus goes out with His disciples after the high priestly prayer, and they go to the other side of the Kidron brook to keep watch. But Judas appears with the detachment of soldiers, officers, and chief priests to arrest Jesus. Jesus is brought before the following men to be judged: Annas, Caiaphas, and Pilate. Before Pilate, Jesus is condemned by the people, who prefer to release Barabbas.

## Observations

These were my observations:

1. **Jesus took responsibility for the persecution, like a true leader, and protected the disciples.** Jesus knew that the guards came to arrest Him and realized that if He did not take full responsibility for the accusations, He could also implicate His disciples. And as He told God the Father in His high priestly prayer asking for protection for His disciples that He had never lost any of the men God gave Him; He could not allow any of the disciples to be persecuted because of Him. Furthermore, it had to happen this way so that His words would be true and be fulfilled. This is a characteristic that in this day and age is rarely seen in leaders who truly ought to adopt it: the leader will be the main one responsible for what happens to their team. Nowadays, everyone wants credit when things go well. But when they go wrong, they want to point fingers at others: this is not the stance of a true leader. A true leader is willing to receive rewards and praise, but is also prepared to accept criticism and responsibility.
2. **Jesus accepted being persecuted in the name of God.** When the guards answered Jesus' question about whom they were looking for and said Jesus of Nazareth and drew near to arrest Him; Simon Peter drew his sword and cut off the right ear of Malchus, one of the servants of the chief priests. But Jesus prohibited him and the other disciples from resisting His arrest. For He was willing to do God's will and did not want to run away from His purpose, even if it cost Him His life. This is one of the most beautiful examples of faithfulness, faith, and conviction in the word of God. For what Jesus did was the hardest thing to do: sacrificing His life to fulfill God's will. Which on this occasion was dying the most painful and humiliating death, being innocent, for sinners. This should lead us to reflect every day on what we are willing to sacrifice to do God's will. For Jesus left us the commandment to love others as He loved us. Which means that we must be willing to sacrifice ourselves for others. For Jesus told us that we must make disciples of all nations and be prepared to suffer persecution for preaching His word and the gospel of salvation. This is the real challenge in the life of a Christian: the moment of persecution for being a Christian where you will have to choose to be faithful to Jesus and God or reject them.
