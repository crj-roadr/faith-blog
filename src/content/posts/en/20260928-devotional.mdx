# Devotional Ephesians 1: Spiritual Blessings and Adoption in Christ

Today's devotional is found in the book of Ephesians, chapter 1, and this is what took place.

**Synopsis:** In this chapter, Paul writes a letter addressed to the churches of Ephesus, explaining how we are blessed spiritually through Jesus Christ, for it was through Christ that we were adopted as children of God. Paul also gives thanks and intercedes in his prayers for the Ephesians because of the love they showed for Jesus Christ and all believers.

## Observations

These were my observations.

1. **We have spiritual blessings through Christ.** When God created the world, He chose us in Christ. He always intended for us to live in holiness and without sin before Christ (Ephesians 1:4). Yet we rebelled and sinned against God — from the time of Adam and Eve until today — and through that we corrupted creation, bringing sin into the world (Genesis 3:8–19). This caused the earth to be cursed by God and the wages of sin to be death (Genesis 2:17). For God, the standard of what is considered good is perfection — i.e., the absence of impurity and sin (Genesis 1:4 and 1 Timothy 4:4). Since all of us are sinners and can never live without sinning, we were condemned to death. Nevertheless, God knew all of this, and through His mercy and justice He already had a plan to save us. He set that plan in motion when He made His promise to Abraham. It was in that promise that God declared all would be saved through a descendant of Abraham — one person in Abraham's family tree (Genesis 12:2–3). And that person, from the very beginning, was Christ. Because God does not contradict His values, a perfect person had to pay for humanity's sins to satisfy both His mercy and His justice. God the Son chose to come into the world in the person known as Jesus Christ to fulfill this perfect sacrifice for us. Through that sacrifice, all of us have the opportunity to be reconciled with God and receive the eternal life we had lost when we first sinned before Jesus came (John 3:16). Moreover, it is through this sacrifice that we also receive God's Spirit within us when we accept Christ as our Lord and Saviour (Ephesians 1:13). As a consequence, we also have the possibility of developing spiritual gifts and fruit.
2. **We must give thanks and pray for all who show their love for Christ and all believers.** This is very important, because Jesus told us before He departed that we must love our neighbour as ourselves. One way to show that love is by giving thanks for someone's life and praying for their spiritual and physical wellbeing. We must also pray and ask God to give them the Spirit of wisdom and revelation so they may know Him better (Ephesians 1:17). Furthermore, Jesus told us that all who do the will of God are His family — brothers, mother, and so on. That means they are our brothers and sisters in Christ. Therefore, if we must do good to everyone, we must give priority to those of the household of faith (Galatians 6:10). Certainly, all of us will face hardships in one way or another throughout our lives, for Jesus told us that in this world we will have trouble (John 16:33). It is therefore our duty to be prepared and clothed in Christ, persevering in faith and praying without ceasing for ourselves and for our brothers and sisters in the faith. For the servant is never greater than his master, and if the world rejected and persecuted Jesus, it will also reject and persecute His followers (John 15:20). Our protector and true safe harbour, therefore, is the Holy Spirit who dwells within us — our Comforter and Guide — who will lead us to God's divine protection.
